# What landed today

Three new cards from three operators across two lanes. Graham Dumpleton on what makes AI-assisted coding actually work. Simon Willison on the non-linear relationship between reasoning effort and creative output quality. Kevin Indig on why Google's listicle demotion targets substitutability, not format.

## The design belongs to the engineer

Graham Dumpleton published Wrapture, a Python library for function wrapping and tracing, with all code written by AI under his direction. Simon Willison highlighted the library on August 31 and quoted Dumpleton's framing of the development approach directly.

The insight is about what domain expertise makes possible. When an engineer has spent years in a specific corner of a language, they know exactly what the output should look like before writing a single line. That prior knowledge translates into precise instructions. Without it, instructions are underspecified and the AI fills the gap by guessing the design, which produces output that requires structural rework. The AI's execution ceiling is high. The constraint is the specificity of the direction it receives.

> "I have spent a long time in this particular corner of Python and knew exactly what the result needed to be, and the AI was the means of producing it rather than the source of the design."

Dumpleton's case is not an argument against using AI to explore unfamiliar domains. That is a different workflow, with different inputs and different expectations. His point is narrower: when you already know the shape of the result, domain expertise is what makes AI execution productive rather than merely fast. `ins_dumpleton-expert-design-ai-executes`.

- Graham Dumpleton, `ins_dumpleton-expert-design-ai-executes`. Knowing the exact shape of the desired output before starting an AI session is what makes domain expertise the real leverage point in AI-assisted development.

## Reasoning effort is a step function, not a dial

Willison tested Claude Fable 5.1 with the same creative prompt at all five reasoning effort levels. The results show a step function, not a gradient. At low and medium, the model skips reasoning entirely for a creative SVG task. At max, it produces 65,927 reasoning tokens at $3.30, with explicit self-correction visible in the trace.

The mechanism: the model decides, based on task and effort setting, whether to invest reasoning tokens. For creative tasks at low and medium, it judges the investment not worth making and goes directly to output. At max, the budget unlocks deliberate iteration. The trace shows the model reconsidering decisions mid-stream rather than committing to an initial choice.

The output difference is qualitative, not just quantitative. The low-effort pelican is a reasonable SVG. The max-effort pelican has a hat, a fish basket, and refined feather structure, each addition traceable to a specific revision step in the reasoning trace. The practical constraint is cost: 33x higher at max than at low. That rules out max effort for any high-volume workflow. For low-volume, high-quality creative work, the investment is now measurable. `ins_willison-reasoning-effort-nonlinear-creative`.

## Substitutability, not format, triggers Google demotion

Kevin Indig published his listicle ranking study on August 24, analyzing 5.32 million organic result rows across 60,000 US English queries in 15 verticals. The pattern: Google trims listicles from top positions without removing the format from results pages. The pages that drop share one property with each other and not with the pages that hold: substitutability. Any of the demoted pages could be swapped for any other covering the same topic and the user would not notice the difference.

This reframes the common interpretation of Google's listicle demotion. The demotion is not an anti-listicle signal. It is an anti-interchangeability signal. A listicle with original survey data, proprietary testing, or editorial selection backed by stated criteria carries a non-substitutability property. That property holds its ranking position regardless of format. Indig operationalized this finding as a Claude skill for pre-publish substitutability scoring, linking the research to a practical diagnostic. `ins_indig-substitutability-not-format`.

- Kevin Indig, `ins_indig-substitutability-not-format`. Google's listicle demotion targets content interchangeable with competitors' coverage; pages with original data, proprietary testing, or distinctive editorial selection criteria hold position regardless of format.
