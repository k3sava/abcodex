# What landed today

Two cards from one operator, one source. Both from Ethan Mollick's "The Overhang" (September 18). The library captured the selection claim from that essay on September 19. These two claims were missed then. They are the vocabulary and agency dimensions of the same overhang argument.

## What you know determines what AI gives you

Mollick's core observation in `ins_mollick-wide-knowledge-unlocks-ai` is that AI encodes patterns from many domains but only surfaces them when the prompt frames the right ask. Wide cross-domain knowledge provides the vocabulary to frame those asks. Narrow specialists, even deep ones, cannot request patterns they cannot name.

The mechanism is not about AI capability but about prompt framing. A person who knows design critique vocabulary can ask for a specific fix. A person who lacks it cannot ask and therefore cannot receive. Mollick is direct on this:

> "AI tends not to volunteer any of these patterns unless you know to ask"

And the flip side:

> "If you do know the right terms, asking for changes is easy."

This claim extends two existing Mollick cards. `ins_mollick-selection-scarce-resource` established that the bottleneck has shifted from production to selection. Wide vocabulary affects selection as much as it affects prompting. `ins_mollick-agent-era-favors-experts` holds that domain expertise increasingly determines what AI can help you do. The vocabulary claim is a concrete mechanism for why.

- Ethan Mollick, `ins_mollick-wide-knowledge-unlocks-ai`. Wide cross-domain knowledge provides the vocabulary to ask AI for patterns it would not volunteer unprompted, making the prompter's knowledge base the binding constraint on AI output quality.

## Agency is the only edge on a flat frontier

`ins_mollick-agency-ai-frontier` addresses a different bottleneck: discovery before documentation. AI capabilities sit latent in models, accessible to anyone who tries the right framing. The window before capabilities are publicly documented is a period of shared confusion. Nobody knows what is possible. That flat uncertainty means agency is the differentiator:

> "a willingness to test the boundaries of what's possible when everybody is equally confused about what AI can do"

The word "everybody" carries the weight. The confusion is not specialist confusion or amateur confusion. It is uniform. Those who experiment in that window find capabilities; those who wait learn from others who did. The overhang is not just about unused production capacity. It is also about undiscovered capability that agency closes before announcements make it common knowledge.

- Ethan Mollick, `ins_mollick-agency-ai-frontier`. Willingness to experiment at the edges of what AI seems able to do is the mechanism by which practitioners discover capabilities before they become general knowledge, because shared confusion makes agency the only differentiating variable.
