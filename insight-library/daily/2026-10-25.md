# What landed today

Four cards, two operators, two converging angles on the same infrastructure moment. Breunig looks at context engineering from inside the system. Tunguz looks at it from the capital stack. Both arrive at the same conclusion: the determinism question is the central design decision, and the economics support building it out.

## Context engineering as an architectural choice

Drew Breunig's October 24 essay traces three years of field evolution, from prompt engineering to context engineering to what he calls harness engineering. The productive frame he introduces in `ins_breunig-deterministic-probabilistic-split` is not about prompts at all. It is about which parts of a system should be fixed in deterministic code and which should remain in the model's probabilistic judgment. His observation is that the field is moving more assembly work out of model calls and into code, making behavior auditable and reproducible rather than variable. The six-tactic toolbox (context augmentation, tool loadout, quarantine, pruning, offloading, summarization) has stayed stable across all three phases even as the problems shifted.

The companion card `ins_breunig-long-context-error-amplification` names the failure mode that motivates this. Context windows amplify errors alongside information. A mistaken early assumption and a correct instruction receive identical treatment from the model. As sessions grow, accumulated wrong turns compound. Breunig cites Gemini 2.5 planning degradation past 100,000 tokens and a study showing thinking tokens per turn fell 75% as sessions extended. His own rule: reset around 200,000 tokens. A forgetting-enabled agent team scored 1,144,695 in the AHC014 study versus 961,136 for a single non-forgetting agent. More context is not always more capability.

> "Long contexts let you fit in more information, but they also let you fit in more errors."

> "We're moving more and more of the work of assembling context from probabilistic model calls to deterministic code."

- Drew Breunig, `ins_breunig-deterministic-probabilistic-split`. Context engineering is an architectural decision about what to fix in code versus what to leave to the model.
- Drew Breunig, `ins_breunig-long-context-error-amplification`. Larger context windows amplify errors alongside information; the fix is pruning and resetting, not adding better information on top.

## GPU economics and the inference payback floor

Tomasz Tunguz published two pieces in the October 18 to 21 window that form a connected argument. The October 21 post builds a four-rung GPU revenue ladder in `ins_tunguz-gpu-inference-payback-ladder`. DeepSeek's theoretical maximum is $38 per card-hour. B200 spot prices trade at $8.77. SpaceX's negotiated enterprise rate, implied from its S-1, is $5.27. SpaceX's realized revenue per card-hour from its 8-K is $2.44, a 93% discount from the theoretical maximum and a 15x efficiency gap from the floor to the ceiling. At every rung, SpaceX CFO Bret Johnsen confirmed on the Q3 2026 call that payback is under one year.

> "At current pricing, new compute deployments are generating a payback of less than a year."

The October 18 post applies that same economic reality to valuation in `ins_tunguz-anthropic-cloud-valuation-frame`. Anthropic's Q2 2026 annualized run rate of $46B grew 1,374% year over year. Its data center commitments of $518B are roughly eight times that run rate. On Ramp's coding-agent buyer list, 87% of customers chose Claude Code. These are cloud-provider patterns, not software-company patterns. Tunguz applies the Microsoft Azure multiple and arrives at a valuation consistent with the current price.

> "If it rains like a cloud & computes like a cloud… That's how we should evaluate Anthropic."

The two cards form a floor-to-ceiling argument. Infrastructure earns back in under a year at even the least-efficient real deployments. At scale, the economics justify cloud-provider positioning. Valuing the category by SaaS comps undershoots what the build-out data already shows.

- Tomasz Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-gpu-inference-payback-ladder`. Sub-1-year payback holds even at 15x efficiency below the theoretical maximum; SpaceX 8-K confirms $2.44 realized per card-hour.
- Tomasz Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-anthropic-cloud-valuation-frame`. Anthropic's revenue growth, capex commitments, and developer tool concentration match cloud-provider patterns; cloud-provider multiples justify the current valuation.
