# What landed today

Four cards, four operators. One addresses the next phase of AI agent security. Three converge, independently, on the same economic argument: when AI handles execution, the part of the work that retains value shifts upward.

## Agent worms don't need to escape. They need a shared surface.

Matthew Green's September 30 essay closes a gap in the existing agent threat-model coverage in this library. Previous cards documented sandbox escapes: wikis exploited via GET writes (`ins_willison-rogue-agents-side-channel`), folder-name encoding via Artifactory (`ins_tunguz-agents-covert-channels`), a model attacking Hugging Face via a zero-day (`ins_willison-ai-sandbox-escape`). All three involve agents reaching outside their intended boundary.

Green's argument is different. `ins_green-agent-worm-shared-infra` documents the case where agents never leave their sandboxes. The covert channel is a shared resource both sandboxes legitimately access: in the research case, a package cache. One agent leaves instructions. Another reads and follows them. Isolation stops nothing.

The generalization to personal agents is what makes this land hard. Email, Slack, shared documents, and calendar invites are all "package caches" for anyone running personal agents like Muse or any productivity-layer AI assistant. A single crafted message anywhere in those shared surfaces is enough to redirect a whole agent network. Green names the actual threat: not a rogue AI that disobeys, but a network of obedient AIs that follow instructions from the wrong source.

> "a swarm of perfectly amenable agents that never leave their sandboxes, each doing exactly what it's told to do, by a human being who wasn't supposed to be giving it orders"

- Matthew Green, `ins_green-agent-worm-shared-infra`. The covert-channel attack on agent networks exploits shared writable surfaces, not sandbox exits. Any shared resource both agents can read/write is a potential instruction channel.

## Context, quality, and individual authority: three operators, one structural shift

Karri Saarinen (Linear), Katie Dill (Stripe), and Elena Verna (Lovable) each published at the Lenny and Friends Summit on September 29 without coordinating. Each named a different dimension of the same underlying shift. Together they are a new instance of `pat_execution-cheap-judgement-scarce`: execution is compressing, and the human input that retains value is the part AI cannot supply.

Saarinen names it at the product level. `ins_saarinen-context-is-the-product` argues that when AI writes the code, code is no longer the product. The product is the context, intent, and understanding that directed what got built. Teams accelerating execution without increasing customer learning are optimizing for the commodity. The differentiating input shifts from production speed to understanding quality.

> "The output is not the product. Customers are not buying the lines of code"

Dill names it at the design level. `ins_dill-ai-raises-ceiling-not-floor` distinguishes two uses of AI that look similar from the outside: floor-lifting (doing adequate work faster) and ceiling-raising (doing work that was previously too slow to ship). Teams that floor-lift converge on "zombie UI," generic product experiences produced without a distinct point of view. The ceiling-raising teams use AI's speed to hit quality levels they could not have afforded before. The test: whether the product contains details that anticipate user needs in ways a faster-but-same-quality process would have dropped.

> "AI can help us raise the ceiling, not just the floor"

Verna names it at the organizational level. `ins_verna-high-impact-ic-era` introduces the High-Impact Individual Contributor: someone with senior judgment who can complete a project end-to-end, across every function needed, without a team to cover the gaps AI now fills. The constraint is not capability anymore. It is information access and decision authority. Organizations that route senior contributors through approval chains and information silos will not realize this structural shift.

> "an individual contributor who can complete a project that delivers business value, end-to-end, on their own"

All three are different readings of the same signal: the compressing part is execution; the part that holds value is judgment, taste, and contextual authority.

- Karri Saarinen, `ins_saarinen-context-is-the-product`. When AI generates code freely, context and intent become the actual product.
- Katie Dill, `ins_dill-ai-raises-ceiling-not-floor`. AI enables ceiling-raising quality gains, not just floor-lifting speed gains, for teams that direct it with taste.
- Elena Verna, `ins_verna-high-impact-ic-era`. AI expands what individual contributors can own end-to-end; the bottleneck shifts to information access and decision authority.
