# What landed today

Four cards, one new operator profile. Two from Tom Tunguz on October 13, one from Kevin Indig on October 12, one from Jake Castillo on October 14. Three independent lanes: AI infrastructure, AI search, and mobile app growth. No synthesis pattern today as the convergences are cross-lane and involve only two operators each.

## AI infrastructure: the competition shifts from model quality to hardware and security

Frontier model intelligence has converged faster than costs have. Tom Tunguz's October 13 analysis of five frontier models found they score within five points of each other on his intelligence index, yet serving costs differ by 3.8x. `ins_tunguz-custom-silicon-cost-moat` traces the gap to custom silicon. Google's TPUs deliver 1.3 to 1.9x better power efficiency than comparable Nvidia hardware (Google-reported figures), which Tunguz estimates translates to approximately 44% lower token serving cost at comparable margins. Google Argon costs $1.99 per intelligence-index task in his analysis, 74% below Fable 5.1 and 67% below Opus 5.5. The deployed capex behind this, $180 to 190B in Google's 2026 guidance, is not quickly replicated.

The second structural pressure came from the same date, a different Tunguz post. `ins_tunguz-security-tooling-gap` draws from Theory's 2026 AI in Practice Survey of 455 senior technical builders, fielded September 22. Security and governance rose from sixth to first among pain points in twelve months: 43% named it as the top problem, with evals second at 22%. The gap between pain and tooling is stark. Only 10% say security tooling delivers the most value today. But 18% expect security tooling to lead in the future, the largest expected gain of any category, pointing to where enterprise AI infrastructure investment flows next.

The two cards share a structural shape: both identify where the model-capability-first era has underinvested, and both name infrastructure (silicon, security) as the corrective layer.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-custom-silicon-cost-moat`. Custom silicon creates a 3.8x cost advantage at intelligence parity, making hardware the emerging moat.
- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-security-tooling-gap`. Security tops pain points at 43% but only 10% say security tooling delivers value, the largest gap in the AI stack.

## AI search: the shortlist is built after the sale, not before it

Kevin Indig's October 12 Growth Memo reframes the path to AI search visibility. `ins_indig-post-purchase-ai-shortlist` The traditional SEO inputs that once predicted organic rank (domain rating, referring domains) correlate at only 0.3 with AI Overview visibility in Indig's Ahrefs data. Post-purchase signals (YouTube mentions, branded web mentions, branded anchors) correlate at 0.6 to 0.7.

The causal model Indig describes: a customer buys, then leaves a review, posts a video, or searches by brand. Those signals feed into the training data that AI search systems use to build shortlists. Shortlist position then determines most of what happens next: 74% of participants in Indig's AI Mode study picked the top-ranked product, and 88% took the shortlist as-is without verifying. AI search is already a shortlist-and-done interaction for most shoppers.

The implication for measurement is significant. Post-purchase signals that drive AI visibility don't show up in last-click attribution. A brand that wins the AI shortlist and sees revenue grow has no standard dashboard view that explains why, which makes post-purchase experience structurally underinvested even as it becomes the primary demand lever.

> "the shortlist gets built from what your customers do after they buy"

- Kevin Indig, `ins_indig-post-purchase-ai-shortlist`. AI shortlist inclusion is driven by post-purchase signals that don't appear in last-click attribution.

## App growth: comments reveal intent, followers do not

Jake Castillo, co-founder of Cal AI, spent thousands on UFC creator posts and saw no download spike. `ins_castillo-influencer-context-conversion` His diagnosis is specific: UFC fans watch fights in social settings, not in the mindset for calorie tracking. "The last thing they want to think about is tracking their calories." He applies the same logic to mukbang creators: their audience is planning a cheat day, not a diet.

The mechanism Castillo names is audience context at the moment of viewing, not niche alignment at the aggregate level. UFC creators have fitness-adjacent audiences. That alignment does not survive the viewing context. Comments reveal what the audience is actually discussing, which is a proxy for the mindset they are in when they engage. Views and followers aggregate across all contexts and hide the mismatch.

> "Views can lie. Followers can lie, but comments cannot."

The finding is a specific failure case from a well-attributed primary source. It doesn't make a claim about all creator-led app growth, only about the subset where the product requires a restrictive or deliberate mindset in an audience primarily in indulgence or entertainment mode.

- Jake Castillo, `ins_castillo-influencer-context-conversion`. Audience context at viewing time determines app conversion; comments reveal intent while views and followers do not.
