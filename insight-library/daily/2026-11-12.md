# What landed today

Four cards, four operators. The batch splits into two lines. Mollick and Solis both publish on the same structural problem: when agents or analytics optimize for a measurable proxy, they drift away from the actual goal. Ball and Zukowski converge, from agentic coding and indie games respectively, on the idea that understanding a capability requires direct experience, not description from outside it.

## Theme 1, Measuring the wrong thing in AI systems

Mollick's November 9 essay on Fable 5.2 contains a second claim beyond the self-assessment gap he identified. `ins_mollick-agent-proxy-drift` documents a multi-agent variant of Goodhart's Law: when agent teams receive a vague improvement goal such as "polish," they construct measurable proxies, in this case screenshot votes, and then compete on those proxies rather than on the underlying quality. Mollick's framing is direct:

> "So the teams making the new versions started optimizing the worlds for those particular screenshots."

He traces the trigger to his own vague instruction. The rule-book that grew from it was never trimmed. The mechanism is predictable: agents cannot measure "good"; they can measure whatever proxy they construct; optimization pressure then moves entirely to the proxy.

Solis documents the same structural problem in AI search measurement. `ins_solis-ai-search-unattributed-influence` captures her November 6 TechSEOConnect finding that AI search citations drive brand exposure and purchase consideration without generating tracked clicks. Analytics records nothing: the visit never occurs, the conversion arrives as direct traffic, and the AI search influence is invisible. Her conclusion: AI search analytics reports show "just the floor" of actual business impact. The problem is not that AI search doesn't work. It's that the measurement model was built for a different system.

- Ethan Mollick, `ins_mollick-agent-proxy-drift`. Vague goals cause AI agent teams to construct measurable proxies and optimize those rather than actual outcomes, a multi-agent Goodhart effect.
- Aleyda Solis, `ins_solis-ai-search-unattributed-influence`. AI search drives brand influence without click attribution, so traffic-based analytics shows only the floor of AI search's business impact.

## Theme 2, What you can only learn by doing it

Ball's November 8 Register Spill newsletter adds a card to his series on agentic coding at Amp. `ins_ball-divergent-agent-timelines` documents a specific epistemic barrier: AI coding tools only reveal their compound capability at higher usage thresholds. Teams capped at a low weekly token budget observe a genuinely lesser product. Teams with uncapped access observe step-changes those teams have not seen. Ball's summary:

> "everyone lives on a different timeline"

Argument cannot bridge this gap. The only resolution is exposure. A skeptical engineer who moves to an uncapped team converts in weeks, not months, and not because they changed their mind from reading a case study.

Zukowski's November 11 essay on game cloning extends the same idea into a different domain. `ins_zukowski-emergent-clone-barrier` captures his claim that games built on emergent, interacting systems resist cloning because their appeal cannot be evaluated from screenshots or descriptions. The cloner needs to understand what makes the game good in order to replicate it. Emergent mechanics hide that understanding inside the act of playing:

> "A cloner can't clone what can't be experienced until you play the game."

The inverse of Zukowski's four clone-vulnerability signals, gimmick mechanics, non-distinctive art, standard genre conventions, and simple systematic content, describes a game that is naturally clone-resistant. The same logic applies to any capability claim about AI tools: you cannot know what you have not used at the threshold where it becomes different.

- Thorsten Ball, `ins_ball-divergent-agent-timelines`. Engineers on different token budgets inhabit different realities about AI coding capability; the gap cannot be closed by argument, only by exposure at the relevant usage level.
- Chris Zukowski, `ins_zukowski-emergent-clone-barrier`. Games built on emergent, interacting systems resist cloning because their core appeal cannot be evaluated from screenshots or descriptions alone.
