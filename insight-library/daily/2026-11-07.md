# What landed today

Three cards, no new operators. Two distinct themes: the physical and economic constraints bounding AI infrastructure growth, and the structural limits of knowing whether AI systems are safe.

## Theme 1, Physical infrastructure bounds AI compute in 2027

`ins_tunguz-power-ai-ceiling-2027` (Tom Tunguz, November 4) names power capacity in gigawatts, not processor supply, as the operative ceiling on AI compute in 2027. The mechanism is a timing mismatch: data centers get built faster than the grid can energize them. Finished server racks sit in warehouses. GPU rental spot prices rise not because chips are scarce but because powered slots are. Tunguz reports US capacity rising from about 38 GW to an 81 GW ceiling in 2027, with Amazon receiving roughly $5B of Nvidia GB300 racks in August 2026 before the substation was ready, and GPU rental rates rising from $3 to $12-13 per hour. The counter case is real: a 27B-parameter Qwen model scored 52 on the Artificial Analysis index versus 51 for a 753B model. If per-task compute requirements fall fast enough, the power ceiling may never bite.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-power-ai-ceiling-2027`. Power capacity, not chip supply, is the binding 2027 AI compute ceiling; stranded racks drive rental rates up faster than chip cost curves predict.

## Theme 2, AI collapses attack economics while alignment verification remains out of reach

`ins_tunguz-ai-attack-cost-collapse` (Tom Tunguz, November 6) documents a 99% cost reduction in enterprise network intrusion between April and September 2026: from roughly $7,500 to $90 for a 32-step compromise. The mechanism is a structural asymmetry. Defenders must inspect every alert at 4,400+ per day; attackers need only one path. AI-assisted investigations run at $2 per alert but historically two-thirds of alerts went uninspected. Attackers using AI swarms now run expected-value calculations: spend compute only on organizations where payout exceeds cost. In a July 2026 Hugging Face intrusion, about 700 AI agents exchanged 70,000+ messages over four and a half days and harvested 136 production keys.

`ins_goedecke-llm-alignment-impossible` (Sean Goedecke, November 2) is the deeper structural problem underneath both cards in this theme. His argument: we cannot know whether an LLM is reliably aligned because LLMs lack fixed goals, and motive does not exist at the detail level required for verification. Three compounding gaps make it irresolvable with current tools. Dangerous behaviors go unlabeled because they are rarely observed before they trigger. Feature labels are semantically underdetermined in the way Kripke's quaddition problem shows: a "kindness" feature might really be "kindness unless condition X." And billions of interacting features per token exceed human interpretive capacity. The practical upshot connects directly to the attack economics card: a system that optimizes for exploitation at the cost level Tunguz documents, running on models where alignment cannot be verified, represents a category of risk that behavioral testing alone cannot contain.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-ai-attack-cost-collapse`. AI agents cut a 32-step enterprise intrusion from $7,500 to $90 in five months, turning attack economics into an expected-value calculation attackers can run at scale.
- Sean Goedecke, `ins_goedecke-llm-alignment-impossible`. Reliable LLM alignment is not merely difficult but structurally unverifiable: no stable goal to align, no interpretable motive at the detail level required, no delegation escape hatch.
