# What landed today

Five new cards from four operators across three lanes. Two themes ran in parallel, from independent angles. The first: as agents own more code, quality assurance moves to production rather than pre-merge. The second: as AI collapses supply across domains, the human edge moves to selection and trust rather than production.

## Production gates move to the right, not away

Thorsten Ball posted on October 13 that his team at Amp has no pull requests and no code review. They push straight to main. The oversight mechanism: agents read production logs, watch deploys, grant feature flags, and check their own work. `ins_ball-production-gate-replaces-pr` is the live workflow form of what Ball argued in September, that code review will die as agents take over implementation. The September post was a prediction. The October post is a practice.

This connects to Geoffrey Huntley's October 13 card (`ins_huntley-precommit-hook-agent-compliance`), already in the corpus. Huntley enforces agent behavior with pre-commit hooks rather than review queues. Both operators are converging on the same structural move: quality assurance moves from human review gates to automated feedback loops, but the position of the gate shifts, not its existence.

- Thorsten Ball, `ins_ball-production-gate-replaces-pr`. Production logs and deploy monitoring replace pre-merge review when agents own most code output.

## Chip ownership determines the AI cost floor

Two cards from Tomasz Tunguz, both published October 13 from the same week of writing. `ins_tunguz-chip-ownership-cost-floor` makes the structural case: five frontier models now score within five points of each other on intelligence benchmarks, yet cost per task varies 3.8x. The reason is silicon. Google's TPU stack, fifteen years in the making, runs tokens 1.2 to 1.7 times faster at 1.3 to 1.9 times lower power than Nvidia A100s. Tunguz estimates that translates to a 44% serving-cost advantage at similar margins. When intelligence is a commodity, the chip stack is the moat.

`ins_tunguz-security-tops-ai-barrier-2026` comes from the same week's survey of 455 senior technical builders. Security and governance rose from sixth to first as the enterprise AI pain point in twelve months, ahead of evals at 22%. Context limitations, the 2025 barrier, appear solved. Production deployment exposed the next ceiling: access control, compliance, data residency. Only 10% say security tooling delivers the most value today against 43% citing it as the top pain point. That gap is a product market.

- Tomasz Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-chip-ownership-cost-floor`. Vertical chip ownership is the structural cost floor when frontier models reach intelligence parity.
- Tomasz Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-security-tops-ai-barrier-2026`. Security rose from sixth to first as the enterprise AI pain point in twelve months, while security tooling remains the category furthest behind demand.

## AI collapses supply; taste and brand fill the gap

Ethan Mollick's October 18 Charter interview, published as he launched Co-Existence, names four attributes that remain distinctly valuable alongside AI: deep domain knowledge, wide associative knowledge, taste, and agency. `ins_mollick-four-human-edge-skills` is the most specific articulation he has published of the human edge, more granular than the selection-as-scarce-resource framing in his September card.

Kevin Indig's October 12 analysis of AI Mode user behavior supplies the empirical grounding from a different direction. `ins_indig-ai-mode-shortlist-acceptance` reports that 88% of users accepted the AI Mode product shortlist without checking. 74% picked the top-ranked item. 64% clicked nothing. The consideration set, which classic search handed back to the buyer, is now pre-collapsed. Brand recognition was cited as a trust signal in 34% of AI Mode sessions versus 23% in classic search. Where Mollick says taste is what humans supply when AI makes production cheap, Indig shows the downstream: buyers lean on shortlist position and brand authority rather than personal evaluation. The human and the market are doing the same thing, delegating selection to a trusted signal.

- Ethan Mollick, `ins_mollick-four-human-edge-skills`. Deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste, and agency are the four attributes that remain distinctly human-valuable as AI makes production abundant.
- Kevin Indig, `ins_indig-ai-mode-shortlist-acceptance`. AI Mode collapses the ecommerce consideration set: 88% of users accept the shortlist as-is, and the top-ranked item captures 74% of selections.
