# What landed today

Three new cards from two operators. One from Kevin Indig on diagnosing AI category-level decline. Two from Hamel Husain on sequencing and cost-cutting in AI product engineering.

## Theme 1, Category repricing vs. competitive re-ranking

Kevin Indig's Growth Intelligence Brief #22 covered 30 days of AI search visibility data ending July 15, 2026. Most of that brief was captured on August 9. One distinct claim was not: the synchronized decline pattern across an entire vertical.

When one brand loses AI visibility and another gains, that is competitive re-ranking. When every brand in a vertical declines simultaneously, with no single competitor absorbing the redistributed share, the AI surface is doing something different. It found a substitute for the category's core function and stopped routing users to any external site. For stock photos, all 9 named brands declined between 8.7% and 44.7% in the same 30-day window. Indig's read: "A decline this broad and synchronized reads as category repricing rather than nine separate site problems." The correct response is a pivot out of the category, not better positioning within it. `ins_indig-category-decline-ai-repricing`.

- Kevin Indig, `ins_indig-category-decline-ai-repricing`. Synchronized AI visibility decline across all brands in a vertical signals the platform found an in-place substitute. The category is being repriced, not re-ranked.

## Theme 2, Sequence and cost in AI product engineering

Hamel Husain published his AI Product Engineering Notes index on August 12, covering the 13-session curriculum he runs as part of the AI Evals for Engineers course. Two distinct claims are extractable.

The first is a sequencing rule. Teams improving AI products tend to jump to post-training when retrieval improvements would close most of their error cases at a fraction of the cost. Husain's hierarchy: retrieval and context first, systems and harness second, post-training only after exhausting both. Five of 13 sessions address context improvements. Post-training requires labeled data, a compute budget, and a retraining loop measured in days to weeks. Retrieval improvements use the same model more effectively at no model cost. The hierarchy reflects cost and reversibility, not opinion. `ins_husain-retrieval-first-hierarchy`.

The second is a routing technique that belongs in the systems-and-harness stage of that sequence. Most classification inputs cluster at the high-confidence end of a small model's probability distribution. For those inputs, the small model's prediction matches the large model's output, and the expensive call adds nothing. A confidence threshold routes high-confidence inputs through and escalates uncertain ones to the large model. Average inference cost falls because the escalation fraction is typically small, and large-model accuracy is preserved on the full task. `ins_husain-model-cascade-routing`.

- Hamel Husain, `ins_husain-retrieval-first-hierarchy`. Retrieval and context improvements should come before systems work and before post-training; each stage is cheaper and faster than the next.
- Hamel Husain, `ins_husain-model-cascade-routing`. Route classification tasks to a small model when confidence is high and a large model when it isn't; average inference cost falls while full-task accuracy holds at the large-model baseline.
