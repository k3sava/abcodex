Ethan Mollick reports a pattern emerging at large enterprises with no management framework yet built for it. Tom Tunguz brings actual demand-side data to a question the AI industry has debated mostly in theory: does cheaper AI reduce spending, or does it drive enough volume that total spend rises?

## Theme 1, The management fight that follows the small team

Mollick's November 24 X thread draws on conversations with firms across manufacturing and financial services. The pattern is consistent enough across different industries that it is worth naming as a structural observation, not a one-off anecdote.

`ins_mollick-small-teams-management-fights` captures the core claim: scattered teams of 2-3 people are doing the work that previously required dozens. The management fight is not about whether the small team's output is good. It is about what the output implies for everyone else. Who owns this work? What happens to the department that used to own it? What does this mean for headcount?

> "Thing I am hearing from a number of big firms, from manufacturers to financial services: we have scattered teams of 2-3 people using AI that are doing the work of dozens of folks in a fraction of the time, creating large-scale management fights over the implications and politic…"

The mechanism is worth stating plainly. Much of large-org headcount exists to coordinate, review, and approve work rather than to produce raw output. A small team with AI doesn't need most of that overhead, and it can cross departmental lines without asking permission. The organization's existing structure assumed that work volume and headcount tracked each other. That assumption is now breaking, and no one has written the playbook for what comes next.

This is a Tier B observation. The evidence is anecdotal. Mollick is reporting from conversations, and the specific numbers ("work of dozens") are unquantified. The pattern holds across different industries, which limits single-company explanations. The 2.43K likes and 191 replies on the original post, with practitioners confirming similar patterns, add signal that this is not isolated.

For operators inside large enterprises: the question is not whether this will happen to you. It is whether you will be the small team, or one of the dozens, or the manager who has to figure out what the fight is actually about.

- Ethan Mollick, `ins_mollick-small-teams-management-fights`. AI-equipped small teams at big firms are delivering at department scale. The conflict is about territory and implication, not output quality. No playbook exists yet.

## Theme 2, Why AI spend compounds rather than deflates

The lightbulb analogy for AI pricing goes like this: as AI gets cheaper and more efficient, we spend less on it, the way homes spend less on lighting as bulbs improve. The copper analogy goes the other way: as copper infrastructure extended, demand grew so fast that total spend rose despite falling per-unit costs. Tunguz's November 24 essay asks which one AI is actually tracking, and brings data to answer it.

`ins_tunguz-tokens-compound-not-deflate` documents the numbers. Token prices fell 43% while token usage grew 11.1x. On the Ramp corporate card panel, a 1% drop in token price drove 4.3% more tokens consumed. On OpenRouter, the same 1% price drop drove 6.8% more tokens. The threshold for total spending to hold flat is elasticity of exactly 1.0x. Both panels are well above it. Total AI spend is rising, not falling.

The supply side confirms this from the other direction. Neocloud GPU rents rose 48% year over year. CoreWeave repriced 30-50% at lease renewal. If AI demand were softening with efficiency gains, data-center capacity would be loosening. It is not.

The Anthropic S-1 provides a third data point. Revenue grew 13.6x to $16.3B in H1 2026. After the Opus 4.5 price cut in November 2025, quarterly sales growth accelerated from 48% to 132%.

Tunguz names the mechanism: falling prices enable workloads that were previously cost-excluded. Those are tasks that couldn't justify the economics at $X but are viable at $0.57X. As each price reduction clears a new threshold, volume surges. Copper-like, not lightbulb-like.

He also names the limits. Both panels have biases he enumerates directly: corporate cards miss enterprise procurement channels, blended prices mix model routing, and cached tokens cost up to 90% less. The watch signals for 2027: spend-to-volume gap narrows, GPU rents and token prices converge, frontier margins fall below 40%. If those happen together, the analogy may shift.

For operators budgeting AI spend: rising token bills alongside falling per-token prices are not a sign of budget mismanagement. They are the expected shape of the market. The question is not whether AI is getting cheaper. It is how fast new workloads are being unlocked to absorb the savings.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-tokens-compound-not-deflate`. Token prices fell 43% while usage grew 11.1x. Elasticity of 4.3-6.8x across two panels confirms AI spend compounds like copper. Supply-side data (GPU rents up 48%) confirms demand is not softening.
