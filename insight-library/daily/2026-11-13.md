# What landed today

Four cards, three operators. Two Tunguz posts in two days map the same terrain from different angles: who captures AI margin and what happens when the entity that backstopped AI buildout loses its chief. Indig and van der Lee arrive at the same finding from different domains: the obvious optimization target turns out to be the wrong one.

## Theme 1, Where AI gross profit actually goes

Tunguz ran the numbers on the full AI value stack in "Who Captures the Most Margin in AI?" (Nov 11). `ins_tunguz-application-layer-ai-margin` captures the finding: at a 40% gross margin, application companies take roughly 40% of the gross-profit pool, ahead of neoclouds (28%) and inference vendors (24%). Power and data-center operators share the remaining 8%. The mechanism is structural. Infrastructure layers sell commodity units. Application companies sell outcomes, and when an outcome is a 70% cost saving for a customer, the application can price on that saving rather than on the underlying compute cost. Tunguz converted every layer's pricing to a common unit (dollars per watt per year) using CoreWeave revenue, Anthropic S-1/A data, and CBRE wholesale rates to build a comparable stack. The practical read: margin protection in AI doesn't come from owning compute. It comes from owning the customer's business outcome.

One day later, Tunguz published the structural context that makes outcome-based pricing feel more urgent. `ins_tunguz-nvidia-ai-central-bank` documents what Jensen Huang's resignation means for AI capital flows. His framing is specific: Nvidia wasn't just a chip supplier. It deployed $61B in startup equity, backstopped $164.5B in lease guarantees, and extended $63.1B in customer receivables. It was operating as the AI economy's central bank. Tunguz's argument is not that the new management is worse. It is that those commitments were partly personal, and their conditional nature is now visible in a way it wasn't before. As he puts it, a central bank chair change doesn't cause an instant crisis. It causes a repricing of risk across every node that relied on the implicit backstop.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-application-layer-ai-margin`. Application companies capture 40% of AI gross profit because outcome-based pricing decouples their margin from compute cost.
- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-nvidia-ai-central-bank`. Nvidia's $164.5B in lease guarantees and $61B in startup equity made Jensen Huang's departure a systemic credit event for AI buildout.

## Theme 2, The optimization target that measurement overturns

Indig ran a Wynter survey of 200 SaaS buyers for his Nov 9 Growth Memo issue on how software buyers use AI. The finding from `ins_indig-ai-shortlist-expansion` is counterintuitive: AI doesn't narrow the buyer's shortlist. It reopens it. Among buyers who already had candidates, 63% added a vendor through AI, often in round two or three of the conversation when initial answers didn't match specific criteria. Buyers filtered on integrations, pricing tiers, and product fit across a median of three to five follow-up messages. The implication for AEO runs against the common optimization assumption. Getting into the first AI answer is necessary but not sufficient. Winning requires passing the follow-up gates, particularly on pricing, integrations, and vertical fit. A vendor that isn't in the first answer can still enter in round two. A vendor that led the first answer can be eliminated in round three.

Van der Lee measured 1,674 of his own agent sessions and found a nearly identical pattern of optimization misdirection in `ins_van-der-lee-agent-cache-token-dominance`. The common intervention when AI development costs feel high is to tell the agent to write shorter replies. His measurement shows that reply output is 0.6% of token usage, while cache reads are 94.2%. Optimizing reply verbosity targets the smallest cost driver by roughly two orders of magnitude. The right lever is context: system prompt length, number of injected files, and context window scope. The finding aligns with Tom Tunguz's Nov 2 harness layer finding (`ins_tunguz-harness-layer-margin-engine`) that input context drives 85.7-98.2% of token volume at the enterprise level. Van der Lee's data is a single developer's sessions, not a fleet measurement, but it confirms the direction and shows the effect holds even at the individual workflow level.

- Kevin Indig, `ins_indig-ai-shortlist-expansion`. AI expands B2B software shortlists rather than narrowing them: 63% of buyers with existing candidates added a vendor mid-conversation.
- Antoine van der Lee, `ins_van-der-lee-agent-cache-token-dominance`. Cache reads account for 94.2% of agent session tokens, making reply-length optimization a minor lever on cost.
