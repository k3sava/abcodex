# What landed today

Two cards, one operator, two posts published October 26. Both map where AI spending concentrates and why. One argues that security has become AI's third product-market fit. The other reads Anthropic's S-1 and finds that the standard valuation vocabulary does not fit AI foundation model companies because the risk lives in commitments, not debt.

## Theme 1, Security joins coding and support as AI's third product-market fit

Tom Tunguz published "The Third Product-Market Fit in AI" on October 26, arguing that security joins coding tools and customer support as the third vertical where AI has achieved genuine PMF. `ins_tunguz-security-third-ai-pmf` captures the mechanism: attackers now use AI to parallelize campaigns across thousands of targets simultaneously. One tracked 5-day campaign extracted 78,330 secrets from 2,186 organizations. Human security teams cannot match that throughput. The only viable defensive response is AI-native tooling operating at the same scale. That dynamic creates the same token-heavy demand pattern that coding autocomplete and customer support bots established.

The evidence is specific. Finance-sector ransomware rose from 156 incidents in 2024 to 65 in Q1 2026 alone, a 76% year-over-year increase. The security market totals $175B growing at 13% annually. Tunguz's survey of 75 enterprise practitioners identified five AI-heavy security categories: SOC, SIEM, pentesting, threat intelligence, and vulnerability management. The claim rests on an asymmetry argument. Attackers can scale; defenders currently cannot without AI.

> "AI can defend as well as it attacks, provided someone pays for the tokens."

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-security-third-ai-pmf`. Security is AI's third PMF vertical, driven by attack parallelization at machine speed that human security teams cannot match.

## Theme 2, AI company financial risk is in commitments, not debt

The same day, Tunguz published his read of Anthropic's S-1. `ins_tunguz-commitment-leverage` pulls the structural insight: AI foundation model companies carry financial risk in long-duration compute commitments, not debt. Tunguz calculates Anthropic's total compute commitments across seven named agreements at $637B, with approximately two-thirds non-cancelable. The $15B revolver is undrawn. Outstanding debt is $6.2B of non-interest-bearing Amazon convertibles. Revenue is consumption-based with no lock-in.

Traditional software companies carry risk in debt instruments that can be renegotiated or refinanced. AI infrastructure commitments are fixed, multi-year, and payable whether customers renew or not. A revenue slowdown does not reduce the obligation. Standard debt-to-equity analysis misses this structure entirely. The valuation vocabulary for AI foundation model companies does not yet exist in any established framework.

> "The leverage is instead in commitments."

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-commitment-leverage`. AI foundation model companies carry their financial risk in non-cancelable compute commitments, not debt, requiring a different analytical frame than standard tech valuation.
