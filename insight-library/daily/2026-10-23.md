# What landed today

Three cards, three lanes. One on what AI citation engines actually favor in brand content. Two on how to work with and around long-running agents, one practical and one diagnostic.

## AI Overviews cite identity content, not commercial pages

Lily Ray's client observation in `ins_lily-ray-about-page-ai-citation-multiplier` is specific enough to change what a brand prioritizes. She found an enterprise brand's About page generating five times more AI Overview impressions than its homepage in Google Search Console's Gen AI report. The page was written for humans, not for AI systems. The mechanism is straightforward: AI citation engines evaluate pages for their ability to answer "who is this company," and About pages that cover company history, people, expertise, and credentials score higher on those signals than commercial pages built for conversion.

This sits alongside her earlier cards. `ins_lily-ray-firsthand-experience-ai-overviews` showed that first-hand experience markers drive incremental AIO outbound link clicks. The About page extends that: at the brand level, the About page is the primary document where those identity and credibility markers live, and its citation advantage reflects that directly.

The implication is narrow and actionable. Brands optimizing for AI search coverage should audit their About page as a priority, not as an afterthought. A thin boilerplate paragraph or a team photo gallery leaves citation volume on the table.

- Lily Ray, `ins_lily-ray-about-page-ai-citation-multiplier`. An enterprise About page generated 5x more AI Overview impressions than the homepage when written as a full identity document rather than marketing copy.

## Agents: wide directives surface latent capability; stuck context prevents it

Simon Willison's Battle Drones post tells two stories from the same project. `ins_willison-undirected-quality-directive` captures the first. He asked Claude to "Make it a LOT more fun" in a single undirected directive for version 4, and the results were dramatic: games undecided past 25 minutes fell from 14% to 0.9%, comeback wins rose from 24% to 46%, and the version added five distinct game systems in one pass. His explanation is precise: specific prompts constrain the agent to the diagnosis the developer already made, while undirected quality directives open the solution space and force the agent to discover root causes through simulation. The game had an AI-vs-AI playtesting harness, and that feedback loop is what made the vague directive work. Without it, the same prompt would have produced arbitrary changes.

This updates `ins_willison-game-loop-agent-limit`, his September finding that agents cannot produce fun games because fun requires a human feedback loop. The Battle Drones case shows a partial substitute: when the quality metric is measurable (completion rates, win distribution), AI-vs-AI simulation can close enough of that loop for an undirected directive to land.

Drew Breunig's context inertia piece in `ins_breunig-context-inertia-agent-fixation` addresses the inverse problem: what happens when an agent does have a direction but the wrong one. He defines context inertia as the failure where a model commits to an early hypothesis and keeps pursuing it after the evidence says to stop. Two mechanisms drive it. Context poisoning: an early wrong idea gets referenced repeatedly and treated as ground truth. Context distraction: as context grows, the model over-indexes on its own accumulated history, and Tan et al. found thinking tokens per turn fell by 75% as sessions grew. Summarization does not fix this because it typically preserves the in-progress work, which is the thing driving the fixation. The only fixes that work follow a reset-and-carry-forward pattern: drop the context, keep only the best solution or a handoff note, and restart.

Willison's undirected directive and Breunig's context inertia address opposite ends of the same question about how to deploy an agent on a quality problem. An undirected directive with a feedback harness gives the agent room to find the right approach. Context inertia describes what happens when the agent found a wrong approach early and no one broke the loop.

- Simon Willison, `ins_willison-undirected-quality-directive`. A vague quality directive produced better game-feel results than specific feature requests because it gave the agent a feedback harness and latitude to discover root causes.
- Drew Breunig, `ins_breunig-context-inertia-agent-fixation`. Context inertia locks agents into early wrong hypotheses as sessions grow; compaction preserves the fixation; only context resets with selective carry-forward break it.
