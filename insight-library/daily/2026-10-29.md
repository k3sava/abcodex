# What landed today

Three cards, three operators, one theme threading through all of them: we are inside an AI inflection that human intuition keeps misreading. One card reads the GitHub numbers. One reads the forecasting record. One reads the product gap that follows from both.

## Theme 1, GitHub's volume surge is a measurement problem, not a productivity story

Simon Willison published his read of GitHub Octoverse 2026 on October 28. The numbers are striking on their face: merged pull requests tripled to 220 million per month, commits more than tripled to 7.38 billion per month, new repositories doubled to 30.3 million per month. `ins_willison-agentic-coding-tripled-github` argues that the right response to these numbers is not to celebrate a productivity boom but to revise what the metrics mean. Volume now tracks agent adoption intensity, not human developer headcount or output. Treating 220 million merged PRs as evidence of 220 million worth of human engineering work misreads the signal entirely.

This matters for anyone using GitHub activity as a proxy for team health, hiring needs, or industry growth. If you are benchmarking your engineering throughput against industry averages, the averages no longer mean what they used to.

> "This is a pretty stark representation of the impact of agentic engineering on GitHub."

- Simon Willison, `ins_willison-agentic-coding-tripled-github`. GitHub volume stats in 2026 are a signal of agent adoption, not human developer output.

## Theme 2, The expert forecasting record on AI is a calibration lesson

On October 20, Ethan Mollick published material tied to his new book Co-Existence. `ins_mollick-experts-underestimate-ai-progress` captures the central empirical case: in 2022, top Metaculus forecasters gave AI a 2.3% chance of winning the International Math Olympiad gold by 2025. The median prediction was 2035. AI domain experts placed the median around 2030. In 2025, both Google and OpenAI reached gold-medal level. The gap between prediction and outcome was not bad luck. It was structural. Human intuition is calibrated for linear change. AI capabilities compound year-over-year in ways that violate that intuition even for experts who are otherwise well-calibrated.

This card reads alongside `ins_mollick-co-existence-phase-shift`, also from Mollick: the capability thresholds that experts placed a decade away arrived in under three years, requiring a new operating frame for knowledge work. The forecasting failure is the precondition for the frame shift.

> "All the experts far underestimated the progress AI would achieve in just a few short years."

- Ethan Mollick, `ins_mollick-experts-underestimate-ai-progress`. Systematic expert underestimation of AI progress traces to intuition calibrated for linear change failing to track exponential capability curves.

## Theme 3, Persistent memory is the next product gap for AI agents

Harrison Chase, co-founder and CEO of LangChain, said in a 20VC podcast on October 17 that persistent memory is the capability that is rare today but will define the next generation of AI agents. `ins_chase-agent-memory-next-frontier` captures why: horizontal agents are interchangeable because they have no privileged knowledge of a specific user across sessions. Memory changes that. An agent that learns from prior interactions can feel like something built for you specifically, which is what drives retention. Chase pointed to Instinct and Muse as consumer agents that have already demonstrated this: their personalization and learning capability is what distinguishes them from generic assistants.

This connects back to Theme 1 in a sideways way. The GitHub numbers show agents doing more work. Chase argues that the next competitive gap is not capability at the task level but personalization across sessions. Volume and throughput are table stakes. The agents that win will be the ones users feel are working for them specifically.

> "One thing about agents that is rare today but will be incredibly common in the next few years is this memory layer."

- Harrison Chase, `ins_chase-agent-memory-next-frontier`. Persistent memory is the defining capability gap for AI agents and the reason current horizontal agents feel generic rather than personal.
