# What landed today

Four cards, no new operators. Two themes divided cleanly: how AI agents expose economic structures that depended on human inertia, and what remains genuinely human once agents clear the operational competence floor.

## Theme 1, AI agents expose profit structures built on human inertia

Retailers discovered this week that blocking AI shopping agents is rational, not merely defensive. Tom Tunguz ran the numbers in `ins_tunguz-agent-checkout-upsell-bypass` and found the mechanism: in consumer retail, the base product is priced near cost while profit concentrates in the upsell layer. Agents bypass that layer entirely. Amazon's advertising business, roughly $68.6B at approximately 50% gross margin, equals its non-AWS operating income. United Airlines' upgrades and fees exceed its operating income. An agent that completes a transaction without encountering the upsell prompt does not just cost Amazon a sale. It removes the profit center. Shopify, which earns on transaction volume regardless of who checks out, is the structural exception and welcoming agents in consequence. The implication for businesses evaluating agent-first distribution: the question is not whether to allow agents, but whether the existing pricing model survives agent-led checkout without redesign.

The same week, Georgi Gerganov shipped llama.cpp v1.0.0, documented in `ins_gerganov-llama-stable-api-ecosystem`. After 3.5 years of rapid iteration, the project adopted semantic versioning. A MAJOR bump signals a breaking API change; a MINOR bump adds compatible features; a PATCH is a bug fix only. Downstream developers who had to track nightly commits can now pin a version and ship. The move is the inverse of the retail-blocking story. Rather than refusing ecosystem participation, stable API contracts invite it. Local AI inference moves from a research tool to a foundation platforms can build on with confidence.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-agent-checkout-upsell-bypass`. Major retailers block AI shopping agents because agent-led checkout bypasses the upsell stack that generates most of their actual profit.
- Georgi Gerganov, `ins_gerganov-llama-stable-api-ecosystem`. Adopting semantic versioning moves llama.cpp from a fast-moving experiment to infrastructure downstream developers can confidently build on.

## Theme 2, What stays human when AI levels the floor

Tom Tunguz reported a KC framework from an Office Hours session in `ins_tunguz-eq-human-differentiator`: when AI makes high-quality operational output broadly accessible, the remaining differentiator is character. KC, OpenAI's Chief People Officer, illustrated this with an oncologist case. Several physicians reached the same diagnosis and proposed the same plan. The patient chose the physician with the better bedside manner. Competence was equivalent; behavior was not. Her direct statement:

> "The differentiator is how you show up as a human."

The claim applies whenever the person is part of the deliverable, not just the output. Leadership, customer success, medicine, sales: in each domain, once AI clears the base competence bar for everyone, integrity and how a person behaves in human interaction become the selection criterion.

Thorsten Ball's Register Spill #106 offers a team-level version of the same constraint in `ins_ball-one-meeting-silent-commit`. Amp runs one scheduled meeting per week. The mechanism that makes it work is a two-to-three minute silent period where each person writes down ambitious shipping goals before stating them aloud. Silent writing forces goal-formation inside the individual before social pressure and reactive matching take over. Ball's constraint:

> "Don't write down things you check off a few hours later."

The structure is sparse by design. When every team member can operate at a high floor independently, the coordination overhead drops. The common thread between this card and KC's claim is that the scarce thing is not output anymore. It is the human-layer quality: intention, accountability, and how you show up.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-eq-human-differentiator`. When AI levels operational competence, character and how a person shows up replace competence as the primary differentiator.
- Thorsten Ball, `ins_ball-one-meeting-silent-commit`. A small software team can run on one weekly meeting when it includes a silent period for writing ambitious shipping goals before stating them aloud.
