# What landed today

Three cards, no new operators. Two voices from different vantage points this week. Lily Ray published new citation data on November 19 showing which surfaces AI search actually pulls from. Benedict Evans published his biannual AI Questions deck on November 18 with two structural claims about how enterprise AI adoption is being measured wrong. The two threads are independent. Together they point at the same underlying shift: AI systems are rewiring where value accumulates, and the organizations watching the wrong metric will not notice until it is too late.

## Theme 1, Real people leave a footprint that optimization cannot fake

Lily Ray's November 19 essay opens with a simple line: "a loophole is not a strategy." She traces 23 years of Google updates and finds one constant. Sites that survived every wave, from Panda through the helpful content era and now into AI search, shared one property: they placed real, named people with verifiable records at the center of their content.

The November 2026 Ahrefs citation analysis makes the structural point concrete. YouTube took 24.5% and Reddit 18.1% of Google AI Overview citations among the top 50 cited domains. Both are platforms where real people publish firsthand knowledge under their own names. In a Semrush study of 325,000 prompts, LinkedIn was the second most cited domain across ChatGPT Search, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity. 59% of its AI-cited content came from individual member profiles, not company pages.

The reason is not mysterious. Real experts accumulate a cross-platform record that cannot be reverse-engineered: years of posts, conference appearances, podcast citations, and third-party references. An AI retrieving a page by a named expert can verify that expert's standing through an established Knowledge Panel and years of consistent commentary. A synthetic persona has no such record. Google formalized this in October 2026 by adding fabricated creator profiles to its harmful content guidance.

> "Being genuinely recommended by other people is the most sustainable way forward."

## Theme 2, Enterprise AI adoption is failing at the measurement layer, not the technology layer

Benedict Evans published his Autumn 2026 AI Questions deck on November 18. Two claims from it are worth pulling out separately because they address different failure modes.

The first, documented in `ins_evans-ai-diffusion-not-transformation`, is the scale-without-change trap. Company-wide AI licences give every employee access to a tool. They do not change the structure of what that employee does with their day, who they report to, what approvals they need, or how their work connects to adjacent teams. Evans traces the same pattern across spreadsheets, browsers, and cloud. The tool spreads first. The structural change that makes the tool transformative comes later, and it looks like a different kind of project. Goldman Sachs CIO data gives the benchmark: public cloud, now two decades old, still runs only about 30% of enterprise workloads. Evans calls the current AI phase "spray-and-pray" and finds about 45% of US workers use AI at work but fewer than 15% daily.

The second claim, documented in `ins_evans-agent-inertia-moat-erosion`, addresses incumbents. Many businesses retain revenue not because they deliver better value but because switching requires effort. Customers compare plans in their heads and decide it is not worth the hassle. AI agents remove that friction. Goldman estimates 5% to 20% of free cash flow for UK mobile operators is at risk from agent-driven plan optimization. Apollo's Torsten Slok raised the possibility of an agentic bank run, with assistants moving cash from accounts earning 0.1% to accounts earning 3 to 5%. Evans frames this as years away from mass impact, with current agent use still in early-adopter territory. The structural logic is already in place, though.

The two findings from Evans read as one argument. Enterprises that measure AI adoption by licence count are treating diffusion as transformation and ignoring what agents will do to their own revenue base. Both mistakes have the same root: measuring what is easy to count instead of what actually changes.

- Benedict Evans, `ins_evans-ai-diffusion-not-transformation`. Deploying AI tools company-wide is not the same as changing how the company works.
- Benedict Evans, `ins_evans-agent-inertia-moat-erosion`. AI agents remove the switching friction that protected incumbent revenue from customers who never got around to leaving.
