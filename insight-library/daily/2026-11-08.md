# What landed today

Three cards, one new operator. Two distinct problems underneath today's batch: the economics of who creates credible content, and the measurement shifts required when AI platforms mediate the research phase before anyone visits a site.

## Theme 1, Performance economics realign creator incentives with purchase outcomes

Hector Serrano built Wispr Flow's UGC program on a simple structural insight: pay creators a cut of ad spend, not a flat rate. `ins_serrano-performance-ugc-creator-alignment` documents the result. A 50-creator roster produces 500 to 600 paid ads per month. Top creators earn over $13,000 per month. The compensation structure works as a filter. A creator willing to accept performance risk has to believe the product actually works for them. Serrano noted that the best creators in his program are what he called "allergic to AI," meaning they avoid AI-generated content in their own work, which is the exact quality that makes them credible advocates for an AI product. The lesson generalizes: when you cannot directly observe authenticity, pricing it in creates a self-selection mechanism that surfaces it.

- Hector Serrano, `ins_serrano-performance-ugc-creator-alignment`. Performance-based UGC pays creators a share of ad spend, selects for authentic advocates, and aligns creator economics with purchase outcomes.

## Theme 2, AI search shifts the measurement problem, not just the traffic

Both Aleyda Solis and Rand Fishkin published this week on what it means to measure correctly when platforms do more of the research work before anyone arrives on a site. Their claims come from different angles and address different parts of the funnel.

`ins_solis-tech-seo-execution-gap` (Aleyda Solis, TechSEOConnect, November 6) is about the internal friction AI tools have not solved. Survey data from the talk shows only 28% of SEOs implement 80% or more of their own recommendations. At the same time, 84% say AI has cut their time on tasks. The gap is diagnostic: AI accelerated knowledge production without touching execution. The recommendation backlog grows faster than teams can clear it. Solis argues the fix is workflow integration with human-in-the-loop approval, not more recommendations.

> "AI execution needs workflow integration, guardrails, Quality alignment, or it will backfire." — Aleyda Solis, TechSEOConnect, November 2026

`ins_fishkin-zero-click-visitor-qualification` (Rand Fishkin, SparkToro, November 1) addresses what happens on the demand side when platforms pre-qualify visitors. If a user exhausts platform resources and then clicks through to a site, they arrive at a later stage of the decision than a 2020-era organic visitor would have. Fishkin documents this from SparkToro's own analytics: conversion rate rising as session volume falls. The implication for measurement is that traffic volume alone understates performance. The KPI stack he proposes separates funnel stages: impressions and engagement on external platforms at the top, branded search and direct visits in the middle, actual sales at the bottom. Neither Solis nor Fishkin coordinates with the other, but both are naming the same underlying condition: AI intermediation forces a measurement rebuild, not just a channel shift.

- Aleyda Solis, `ins_solis-tech-seo-execution-gap`. Only 28% of SEO teams implement 80%+ of their recommendations; execution friction is now the binding constraint, not knowledge.
- Rand Fishkin, `ins_fishkin-zero-click-visitor-qualification`. Conversion rates may rise as traffic falls because platform-side research pre-qualifies arriving visitors; site visits have become decision-confirmation steps.
