## Hamel Husain adds failure-mode discovery to the eval workflow

Husain updated "Evals Skills for Coding Agents" in August 2026 with two new skills that address a sequencing problem common in AI engineering teams: applying automated evaluation before the team has any taxonomy of what is going wrong.

The error-discovery skill reverses the standard order. A practitioner provides raw LLM outputs or traces. An agent builds a review application with intelligent sampling and, as the practitioner annotates examples, groups the annotations into named failure modes. The session ends with a typed taxonomy of failures rather than a score with no interpretable categories. A companion start routing skill handles the first decision: it reads the practitioner's situation and directs them to either an eval-audit path (if an existing eval pipeline is in place) or error-discovery (if raw unanalyzed traces are the starting point). The mechanism prevents a recurring anti-pattern where teams reach for automation before they understand what they are measuring. This extends Husain's earlier claim in `ins_husain-product-before-evals` that evaluation failures usually trace to design problems upstream of the model, and connects to `ins_hamel-eval-is-product-smell` where evals function as a signal about product quality. `ins_husain-triage-before-eval-tooling`.
