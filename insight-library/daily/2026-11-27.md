# What landed today

Three cards across two operators from the search and AI-native lanes. The through-line is not a theme in the abstract. It is a pattern across different domains: the places where the gap between AI capability and human outcomes is structural, not solvable by better prompting.

## Theme 1, Where AI search sources trust

Lily Ray's November 20 Search Engine Journal essay is a synthesis piece, not a case study. Its value is that it names where AI search is actually pulling citation signal from, using real data from Ahrefs and Semrush published in November 2026.

The finding is specific. Among the top 50 cited domains in Google AI Overviews, YouTube held 24.5% and Reddit 18.1%. On LinkedIn, 59% of citations in ChatGPT Search and AI Mode came from individual members, not company pages. These are not predictions; they are measurements of the current citation ecosystem.

The mechanism Ray names: AI systems extract reputation signals from years of accumulated third-party content. What independent sources say about you outweighs what you say about yourself. Google's rater guidelines confirm this explicitly.

> "When there is disagreement between what the website says about itself and what reputable independent sources say about the website, we'll trust the independent sources."

This extends the July 2026 Lily Ray finding (`ins_lily-ray-self-promo-recommendation-exclusion`) that self-promotional listicles are cited but excluded from the recommendation. The new card adds the affirmative: not just what fails, but what works. Individual voices in community platforms, practitioners with years of verifiable output, real authors with named bylines across independent sites. These are the sources AI search trusts.

Consumer trust data makes this more urgent: a Gartner 2026 survey found a majority of U.S. consumers say AI content makes them less trusting of brand messaging. 65% of consumers treating AI content as a trust negative makes authentic third-party recommendation more valuable, not less.

- Lily Ray, `ins_lily-ray-external-trust-ai-search-durability`. Third-party trust beats self-description in AI search. YouTube, Reddit, and individual LinkedIn voices dominate AI citation sourcing. Reputation can't be mass-produced.

## Theme 2, The implementation gap AI didn't close

Aleyda Solis's November 13 Tech SEO Connect deck reported the SEOFOMO 2026 Technical SEO survey. The headline stat is on slide 33: only 28% of SEOs get 80% or more of their technical recommendations implemented.

The deck also found that 84% of SEOs say AI has let them spend less time on the tasks they use it for. These two numbers belong together. AI sped up analysis. The implementation gap held steady.

The mechanism is structural. Technical SEO recommendations fail at delivery because the constraint is developer bandwidth, stakeholder buy-in, and CMS limitations. None of those change when analysis gets faster. The bottleneck moved back one step: from "we can't analyze fast enough" to "we can't ship what we've already analyzed." Same queue size, different label on the front of it.

Only 5% of SEOs have let AI apply changes without human approval. The rest still require a human to review, a ticket to be written, and an engineering sprint to run. AI did not change that chain.

For operators running SEO at any scale: the question is not "how do I find more recommendations?" It is "how do I improve my implementation rate?" Those are different problems, and they require different interventions.

- Aleyda Solis, `ins_aleyda-solis-implementation-bottleneck-tech-seo`. AI sped up tech SEO analysis without moving the implementation rate. Only 28% of SEOs get 80%+ of recommendations shipped.

## Theme 3, The attention cost of teaching taste

Dan Shipper, speaking through Every's Context Window newsletter on November 13, described what he calls the most important formula in AI: how much a model learns per unit of human attention.

The formula names the failure modes first. Quick feedback (a thumbs-up) gives too little signal. Detailed feedback demands more effort than most people will give. Both fail because the attention cost exceeds the return. The person stops.

The productive case is an AI that reads your prior work and asks a pointed question, one that reshapes how you think about your own thinking. That exchange pays the person back in kind. They get a sharper question. The model gets a richer training signal. At that exchange rate, the feedback loop runs.

This extends the existing Shipper thread in the library. `ins_shipper-judgment-scarce-generation-cheap` argues that generation is cheap but ranking is scarce, and that encoding human judgment into AI is valuable. This card adds the constraint: encoding that judgment requires a feedback loop that returns value to the person doing the encoding. Without that return, the loop stops. No loop, no encoding.

The full formula derivation is at frontier.every.to and was not retrieved for this card. This card is Tier C: the mechanism is stated clearly, the evidence is conceptual rather than empirical.

- Dan Shipper, `ins_shipper-attention-per-learning-unit`. Teaching AI your taste fails unless the feedback loop returns value in the same exchange. The measure is model learning per unit of human attention.
