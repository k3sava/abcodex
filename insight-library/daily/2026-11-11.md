# What landed today

Four cards, two operators. The batch splits cleanly into two lines: how to price and supply AI tokens correctly at the task level and the infrastructure level, and what happens when organizations and systems embed AI without defining the right constraints. Willison and Tunguz publish from different vantage points, but the week's output converges on a single premise: the wrong unit of analysis produces the wrong decision.

## Theme 1, Measuring AI costs correctly at the task and infrastructure scale

Willison's Nov 10 post argues that price-per-token is the wrong comparison unit for reasoning models. `ins_willison-cost-per-task-reasoning-metric` documents the mechanism: reasoning models generate variable numbers of internal thinking tokens per task, so a model with a lower list price may cost more per completed job. Artificial Analysis publishes a cost-per-task metric that normalizes this. Their Pareto frontier chart shows the boundary where no model simultaneously offers lower cost and higher capability. Willison's framing is blunt:

> "comparing price-per-token doesn't really make sense these days"

The same mismatch between nominal price and actual spend appears at the infrastructure scale. Tunguz's Nov 9 post models when US domestic token production becomes insufficient. `ins_tunguz-token-import-threshold` captures the arithmetic: hardware efficiency compounds at roughly 2.9x per year (blending NVIDIA Vera Rubin at 5.4x, Google Ironwood at 2x, AWS Trainium3 at 4x) but US committed power grows more slowly, from 31 GW in 2025 to 41 GW in 2026 to 66 GW in 2027. At a 45% data center cancellation rate, domestic supply falls short in 2028. At 20%, the gap opens in 2029. At zero cancellations, it holds through 2030. After that threshold, Tunguz's conclusion is structural:

> "tokens will simply be produced elsewhere & imported"

The latency constraint (88ms) limits which workloads can run on overseas infrastructure. Latency-sensitive inference stays domestic; batch and background workloads are candidates for import once the crossover arrives. Both cards rest on the same underlying principle: measure the variable that actually determines cost (task completion, not token count; power availability, not headline GPU deployment numbers).

- Simon Willison, `ins_willison-cost-per-task-reasoning-metric`. Cost-per-task on a standard benchmark, not price-per-token, is the correct unit for comparing AI reasoning models because reasoning token counts vary dramatically across models.
- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-token-import-threshold`. US token production becomes insufficient between 2028 and 2030 depending on data center cancellation rates; workloads tolerating 88ms latency will be served by overseas capacity after the crossover.

## Theme 2, What AI changes in organizations and systems when the constraints are set correctly

Tunguz's Nov 10 post introduces the efficiency-versus-reimagination distinction via KC, OpenAI CPO. `ins_tunguz-reimagination-beats-efficiency` captures the competitive argument: efficiency gains are additive (a 10-hour task becomes a 2-hour task, freeing 8 hours), while reimagination bends the growth trajectory itself. Teams that use AI to attempt work that was previously out of reach compound their output differently than teams that use AI to do current work faster. The gap widens quarter by quarter. Tunguz ties this to the "junior talent" observation: 3 months of current AI-tool fluency now beats 2 years of stale expertise, because the harnesses (Codex, Claude Code) provide domain-level skills, tools, and runtime context that flatten the ceiling on what a generalist can ship.

Willison's Nov 4 post makes an adjacent point at the system level. `ins_willison-llm-app-least-privilege` documents a design constraint he shipped with Datasette Apps: LLM-generated data applications are scoped at creation time to named stored queries only. The agent validates that each referenced query exists and is viewable before the app is created. Write queries require an additional human approval step. The LLM generates code against a constrained surface; even if the generated code attempts to exceed scope, the architecture does not permit it. This is the same logic as reimagination-versus-efficiency at the design level: define the surface the agent operates against rather than letting the agent discover it.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-reimagination-beats-efficiency`. AI adds to earnings arithmetically when used for efficiency; it compounds geometrically when used to reimagine what gets attempted. The gap between the two types of adopters widens with each cycle.
- Simon Willison, `ins_willison-llm-app-least-privilege`. LLM-generated apps scoped to named stored queries at creation time, with write queries requiring explicit human approval, prevent generated code from accessing or modifying data outside intended scope.
