# What landed today

Four cards from one operator, one post. Chris Zukowski scraped all 7,240 games in the October 2026 Steam Next Fest and ran the numbers. The results sharpen the SNF picture considerably. Two themes connect across the four cards: the festival has a structural timing mechanism that most developers don't know exists, and the metrics most developers use to gauge their readiness are not the ones that predict rank.

## SNF has a visible structure, and Wednesday is the pivot

Two cards this week describe how the festival actually works as an algorithm, not just as a promotional window.

`ins_zukowski-snf-wednesday-algorithm-switch` names the timing mechanism Zukowski has observed across multiple SNF editions. For the first one to two days, Valve gives every game a minimum randomized front-page placement. On the first Wednesday, it switches to merit-based ranking, concentrating visibility on games already earning the most demo plays and wishlist activity. The festival does not run as an even six-day window. It runs as two phases: signal collection, then amplification. A game entering SNF without a pre-built audience gets one chance to collect enough signal in the first 48 hours to survive the Wednesday transition. Most don't.

> "On the first Wednesday of SNF, Valve switches from giving all games a baseline of visibility to giving visibility to top-performing games."

`ins_zukowski-snf-demo-freshness-rank-correlation` adds a correlated pattern. Top-25 games had demos live for an average of 18.8 days before the festival, while games ranked 101 or lower averaged 36.6 days. The monotonic decline across all four rank tiers is consistent with a recency mechanism: a newly-launched demo benefits from Valve's fresh-content browse queues, which coincide with SNF if the demo launches two to three weeks before the festival opens. Developers who launched demos earlier in development, without SNF timing in mind, show up in the longer averages and lower ranks.

- Chris Zukowski, `ins_zukowski-snf-wednesday-algorithm-switch`. Valve's SNF algorithm shifts from randomized minimum to merit-based ranking on the first Wednesday; pre-festival preparation, not festival-week actions, determines rank.
- Chris Zukowski, `ins_zukowski-snf-demo-freshness-rank-correlation`. Games ranked in the top 25 had demos live a median of 18.8 days before SNF start, versus 36.6 days for games ranked 101 or lower.

## The metrics that look like signal are noise, and the AI slop is already self-sorting

Two more cards from the same dataset describe what the data actually predicts.

`ins_zukowski-snf-percentage-growth-trap` is the most directly actionable. Percentage follower growth in the week before SNF correlated at Spearman rho -0.13 with peak demo CCU. Absolute follower count (rho 0.55) and raw gain (rho 0.52) were the reliable predictors. The paradox in the data is clean: games ranked 101 or lower showed the highest percentage growth at 12.5%, while top-25 games showed only 7.0% growth. A game with 15 followers gaining 12.5% adds fewer than two followers. A game with 5,519 followers gaining 7.0% adds roughly 386. Percentage growth is a vanity metric that flatters underfollowed games and hides their disadvantage.

`ins_zukowski-ai-disclosure-tier-stratification-oct26` extends the June 2026 temporal filter finding with cross-sectional rank data. In October 2026, 35% of the 6,942 games ranked 101 or lower disclosed AI use, versus 8% in the top 25. The stratification is monotonic across all four tiers. The mechanism is likely behavioral: players send short session-length and low-wishlist-conversion signals that suppress AI-disclosed games before Wednesday's merit transition. Genre confounding is real, AI-heavy genres (Strategy, RTS, Tactical RPG) attract narrower audiences, but the rank outcome is consistent. AI slop is not ruining SNF for players who arrive on day three. It has already sorted itself to the bottom of the rank distribution by then.

- Chris Zukowski, `ins_zukowski-snf-percentage-growth-trap`. Pre-SNF follower percentage growth (rho -0.13) is negatively correlated with festival performance; absolute base (0.55) and raw gain (0.52) are what actually predict rank.
- Chris Zukowski, `ins_zukowski-ai-disclosure-tier-stratification-oct26`. AI-disclosing games make up 8% of the top 25 and 35% of games ranked 101 or lower in October 2026 SNF, confirming rank-level stratification alongside the known temporal filter.
