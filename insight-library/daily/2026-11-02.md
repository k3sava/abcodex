# What landed today

Three cards, two operators. Breunig's October 24 context engineering essay completes its run in the library with two more failure modes. Tunguz maps a structural tension in hyperscaler strategy that Q3 earnings made visible. One theme is about AI systems and their failure patterns. The other is about the business model race that determines who gets to build them at scale.

## Theme 1, Prompts repeat and shout because model training resists what you are asking for

Drew Breunig named six context failure modes in "The State of Context Engineering." Four were captured in previous runs. Two remain, and both showed up in the source again on a fresh read.

`ins_breunig-training-priors-resist-runtime-instructions` names what Breunig calls "fighting the weights." A model's training environment shaped its behavioral defaults. When runtime instructions ask for different behavior, the weights pull back toward the trained pattern. The signal is in the prompt itself.

> "When you crack open a prompt and see repetition and all-caps, there's a good chance you're fighting the weights."

Claude Code's system prompt repeats the instruction to use parallel tool calls across multiple lines. The repetition is evidence that training ran serial and someone kept adding emphasis to override it. Smaller models show this more clearly: they botch the first attempt at a Playwright MCP call before recovering, because their training saw similar tools with different API shapes.

> "The environment it was trained in had similar tools that looked different than Playwright."

The transferable diagnostic: repetition in your own prompts tells you where your training environment and your instruction environment diverge. That gap is the cost you pay at inference time until you work around it or upgrade the model.

`ins_breunig-context-poisoning-hallucination-locks-goal` covers the second uncaptured failure mode. A hallucinated item that enters the agent's working context gets treated as established observation, not as a claim to verify. The agent attempts to satisfy the constraint rather than question it. The example is a Gemini 2.5 session playing a Pokémon FireRed game. The model hallucinated iced tea from a different game version, added it to its to-do list, and then could not proceed because FireRed has no iced tea.

> "Once it had been added to its to-do list, the model could not get past it."

The previous card `ins_breunig-long-context-error-amplification` captured the general principle: larger contexts amplify errors. Context poisoning is the specific mechanism where a single error becomes a blocking constraint. The fix is removal, not correction: reasoning over a poisoned entry does not dislodge it.

- Drew Breunig, `ins_breunig-training-priors-resist-runtime-instructions`. Training-era behavioral defaults resist runtime instructions, and the cost shows as first-attempt failures and required prompt repetition.
- Drew Breunig, `ins_breunig-context-poisoning-hallucination-locks-goal`. A hallucinated fact embedded in agent context becomes an unresolvable constraint, blocking goal completion until the context is cleared.

## Theme 2, The hyperscaler that builds its new engine before AI erodes its funding base wins

Tom Tunguz published "Crossing the Trillion Dollar Chasm" on October 29, the day before Q3 earnings season closed for major cloud providers. `ins_tunguz-legacy-funding-capex-race` pulls the structural claim: hyperscalers fund AI compute capex from legacy business cash flows, while AI adoption simultaneously compresses yield in those same businesses.

The Q3 numbers make the race visible. Google Search growth fell from 19% in Q1 to 12% in Q3 as AI Mode queries monetize below classic search. Amazon Advertising slowed from 26% in Q2 to 12% in Q3 as shopping agents route around its product pages. Meanwhile, Alphabet's free cash flow turned negative at -$9.1B on $51.4B capex. Amazon's trailing twelve-month free cash flow sits at -$42.4B on $77.3B capex.

Microsoft is the exception. Enterprise ARPU rose through AI faster than seat growth slowed, leaving its cash engine intact with $18.6B free cash flow on $45.2B capex. Its funding loop is not under the same pressure. ServiceNow fell 29.6% across two days in late October on fears of AI erosion of seat-based pricing, which traces the same concern one layer down the stack.

The diagnostic Tunguz applies generalizes past hyperscalers. Any company using current business cash flows to fund a platform transformation faces this race if the transformation also compresses yield in the current business. The question is whether the new engine reaches self-sustaining scale before the funding base weakens. Both cards map how AI infrastructure finances differ from conventional software risk: `ins_tunguz-commitment-leverage` covers the commit-structure reading of Anthropic's S-1, and this card maps the business-model race that determines whether the capex runs long enough to matter.

- Tom Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-legacy-funding-capex-race`. Hyperscalers race to reach trillion-dollar compute scale before AI yield compression in their legacy businesses starves the capex that funds the buildout.
