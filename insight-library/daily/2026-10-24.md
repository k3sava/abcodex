# What landed today

Three cards, three lanes, one shared undercurrent: the pace of change in AI is compressing cycles that used to take years into months or quarters. Build-out cycles, planning cycles, benchmark timescales.

## The infrastructure build-out is running on a different clock

Tomasz Tunguz applied Carlota Perez's two-phase build-out model to the AI era in `ins_tunguz-ai-perez-buildout-double-speed`. Perez's model holds that every major technology wave runs infrastructure capital far ahead of application revenue, then waits for applications to catch up and pay for it. The web era took seven years for application revenue to reach 10% of telecom capex. AI hit that milestone in four years, roughly half the time.

The data Tunguz uses is specific: approximately $1.5T of web-era telecom capex against approximately $180B of application revenue over 1996 to 2005. For AI, SpaceX S-1 filings show new compute paying back in under one year at spot prices. Venture investment in AI's first four years ran to approximately $1.24T, a 6.2x ratio versus the web era's first four years.

The qualification he raises is worth noting. Ramp data show 80% of the largest AI spenders are venture-backed, and venture-backed companies now represent over 13% of US employment. A significant portion of AI application revenue may be circulating among startups rather than flowing from the broader economy, which would overstate real-world penetration. The circle, as Tunguz puts it, is a sixth of the economy.

- Tomasz Tunguz, `ins_tunguz-ai-perez-buildout-double-speed`. AI application revenue hit 10% of infrastructure capex in four years vs. seven for telecom; higher VC circularity is the main caveat.

## AI tools push quotas up and compress comp cycles down

Bardia Shahali built Fireworks AI's sales function from $3M to $400M and now runs sales at Granola. His claims in `ins_shahali-ai-sales-quota-amplification` are grounded in that trajectory. AI tools handle more of the research, outreach, and follow-up work per deal, raising what each rep can carry in quota by a meaningful multiple. Headcount stays lean. Deal sizes grow.

The flip side: model capabilities shift every 45 days, which breaks annual compensation planning. Shahali moved to quarterly comp resets because the competitive landscape, deal structures, and buyer criteria all change fast enough that annual plans become stale before the year ends.

> "Each rep, the quota they can carry is just multiples higher."

> "Now we're setting comp every quarter because things change so much from quarter to quarter."

He also distinguishes infrastructure AI sales from application AI sales. Infrastructure deals can close at eight figures in two weeks with forward-deployed engineers alongside account executives. Application-layer sales stays closer to traditional SaaS motions, with account executives and customer success managers, though with higher per-rep productivity. Small teams in either case tend to underinvest in outbound prospecting, a gap Shahali names directly.

- Bardia Shahali, `ins_shahali-ai-sales-quota-amplification`. AI amplifies rep throughput to multiples-higher quotas; the 45-day model-refresh cycle forces quarterly comp resets in infrastructure AI sales.

## A benchmark that saturated is not a ceiling

Ethan Mollick's October 20 essay tracked 30 months of AI progress in charts. The sharpest data point is the METR Long Horizon Benchmark in `ins_mollick-task-horizon-metr-doubling`. AI's task duration at 50% success doubled eight times from GPT-4's release, advancing from roughly four-minute tasks in 2024 to roughly 17 hours by April 2026. Then the benchmark saturated.

Benchmark saturation means the test could no longer discriminate capability at the frontier, not that progress stopped. Separate studies Mollick cites suggest the achievable horizon may extend to 17 weeks. The 50% success caveat matters: AI completes these tasks half the time, not reliably. But the exponential itself is measurable and has held for eight doublings.

Alongside task duration, Mollick notes cost compression: a correct answer on a GPQA-equivalent benchmark fell from about 30 cents to about 0.04 cents over the same period. Task duration and cost per correct answer are moving in opposite directions on the same curve.

This card extends `ins_mollick-14h-agent-run-cost` from June 2026, which captured the 14-hour benchmark from a different Mollick essay. The October data places that number in a doubling-curve context and adds the saturation event and the 17-week estimate from downstream research.

- Ethan Mollick, `ins_mollick-task-horizon-metr-doubling`. METR Long Horizon Benchmark shows eight doublings from 4-minute to 17-hour tasks at 50% success; benchmark saturation signals the frontier exceeded the test, not that progress stopped.
