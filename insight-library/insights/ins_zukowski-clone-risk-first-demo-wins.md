# AI clone risk is conditional on three factors; the first quality demo compounds a lead that even the original idea cannot recover

## Claim
AI cloning only threatens indie games that combine a non-bespoke art style, a viral fantasy, and a well-explored genre. When those three conditions align, getting a quality demo out before competitors is the only defense that works, because Steam's momentum algorithm makes first-mover advantage compound.

## Mechanism
Steam promotes games with existing momentum and hides games with none. A public demo gives content creators something to play and record, which grows follower counts, which feeds Steam's recommendation engine with more signal. A clone that gets a demo in front of creators first captures that flywheel before the original does. The original loses not by being inferior but by being absent from creator discovery.

## Conditions
Holds when: the game's concept is viral and genre-conventional enough for a clone to be plausible substitutes, and the original delays demo release.
Fails when: the game has a bespoke art style or a deeply original mechanic that makes clones obviously inferior. Also fails for most games: Zukowski estimates 99% of indie games do not meet all three cloning risk conditions.

## Evidence
Zukowski tracked Steam follower counts for the Haystack game cluster from August 22 to October 28, 2026. The original game had roughly 23,000 closed playtest sign-ups but no public demo. A clone called Find The Needle shipped a quality public demo before the original; content creators played it, and the clone passed the original in follower count by October 5. A third clone launched a month later with 452 followers and flatlined. Only three Haystack games exceeded 100 followers. The original was gaining 600-plus wishlists per day without a demo, but Zukowski estimated it had roughly 20,000 fewer wishlists than the lead clone.

> "Steam is good at promoting games that already have momentum and really good at hiding games with none."

## Signals
- Clone follower counts exceed the original within two weeks of a clone demo launch.
- Your game has gone viral on social media before you have a public demo available.
- Multiple teams announce games with similar concepts within days of your viral moment.

## Counter-evidence
The Haystack case is one data point. Other viral game ideas have not attracted clones that overtook the original. Zukowski's three-condition filter is conceptual, not validated across a large sample. Games with extremely strong community attachment may retain loyalty even when a clone ships first.
