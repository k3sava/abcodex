# AI Mode generates passive recommendation acceptance while AI Overviews preserve active evaluation behavior

## Claim
AI Mode and AI Overviews produce fundamentally different user behaviors: AI Mode produces passive acceptance of recommendations 88% of the time, while AI Overviews trigger the classic click-evaluate-compare loop that traditional search built.

## Mechanism
AI Mode presents its answer as a terminal output with no embedded links to follow. Users receive a recommendation and have no obvious path to verify it against alternatives, so they accept it. AI Overviews retain the link-grid format users learned from classic Google: the overview summarizes but the links invite evaluation. Users click, compare sources, and verify. The surface format determines the behavioral mode, not the underlying query intent.

Indig states the implication directly: "AEO/GEO is a brand channel disguised as a performance channel. AI recommendations shape demand (not citations)." In AI Mode, the recommendation IS the result. Getting named matters more than earning the click, because the click is not part of the user flow.

## Conditions
Holds when: users interact with Google AI Mode on product or purchase-intent queries. The 88% acceptance figure comes from Indig's user behavior research series covering H1 2026.

Fails when: the query is high-stakes, the user is already skeptical of the recommendation, or the AI Mode result surfaces evidence of uncertainty (e.g., conflicting information in the response). Sophisticated buyers in B2B contexts are more likely to override and verify.

## Evidence
Indig's H1 2026 Halftime Report documents two behavioral regimes:

> "88% of the time, users accepted AI Mode product recommendations as best there is. In AI Overviews, on the other hand, users click, evaluate, and compare a lot. That's classic search behavior, but it doesn't carry over to AI chatbots."

The contrasting behaviors reveal a surface-format effect. The same underlying information, packaged as an agentic answer vs. a linked overview, produces radically different user action rates.

Adjacent data from the same report: close to 75% of consumers pick the number one result in an AI shortlist. Trusted brands override position, but only when the user is actively evaluating alternatives. In AI Mode, most users never reach evaluation.

## Signals
- Brands seeing high AI Mode mention rates but flat referral traffic have entered the passive acceptance regime.
- Brands seeing AI Mode mentions AND click-through need to evaluate whether they are winning on evaluation or on brand trust alone.
- Attribution tools that don't distinguish AI Mode mentions from AI Overviews citations are conflating two different commercial outcomes.

## Counter-evidence
Indig's 88% figure comes from his own user behavior research series, not a named third-party study. The sample composition (consumer demographics, query categories) is not fully disclosed in the report. B2B purchase behavior may not follow the same passive-acceptance pattern, since multi-stakeholder decisions require documentation and comparison by design.
