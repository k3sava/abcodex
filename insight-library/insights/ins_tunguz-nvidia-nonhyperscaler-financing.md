# NVIDIA's AR growth outpaced revenue 3.5x, revealing vendor financing for non-hyperscaler demand

## Claim
NVIDIA's Q2 FY27 results show that non-hyperscaler AI buyers (startups and neoclouds) are now driving incremental demand, but they cannot self-fund GPU purchases at scale, so NVIDIA is extending payment terms and acting as their lender. The mismatch between accounts receivable growth (64% sequential) and revenue growth (18% sequential) in a single quarter signals the size of that implicit credit position.

## Mechanism
Hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon) have cash reserves large enough to prepay for GPU orders. Non-hyperscalers, the ACIE segment in NVIDIA's reporting, do not. When NVIDIA needs the ACIE segment to grow to sustain its own revenue trajectory, it has two options: wait for those customers to raise capital, or extend payment terms. NVIDIA chose the latter. Days Sales Outstanding jumped from 45 to 60 days in one quarter. Accounts receivable surged to $63 billion. Revenue grew 18% sequentially. Receivables grew 64% sequentially.

The divergence is the tell. When AR grows at 3.5x the pace of revenue, the supplier is financing the buyer. NVIDIA's $581 billion in supply commitments and $101 billion in equity stakes in AI startups make the same point from another angle: NVIDIA is invested in keeping this customer segment solvent.

> "Hyperscale revenue more than doubled from a year ago and increased 13% sequentially…"

ACIE (non-hyperscaler) grew 25% sequentially and 138% year over year. The ACIE segment is now the growth engine, but it runs on NVIDIA's credit.

## Conditions
Holds when: non-hyperscaler demand is a meaningful share of NVIDIA's growth trajectory and those customers have limited access to cheap capital.

Changes if: AI startup valuations collapse and NVIDIA's receivables prove uncollectable; or if hyperscalers re-accelerate capex and displace the ACIE growth contribution.

## Evidence
Q2 FY27 results: $96 billion in revenue (106% YoY, 18% sequential). Q3 guidance: $108 billion (±2%). Gross margins: 75.0%.

Days Sales Outstanding: 45 days in Q1, 60 days in Q2. Accounts receivable: $63 billion, up 64% sequentially against 18% revenue growth. Supply commitments: $581 billion. Equity stakes in AI startups: $101 billion.

NVIDIA CFO Colette Kress cited "extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers" as the explanation. Jensen Huang, on the customer mix: "This time last year, one lab alone was driving the buildout; today, we have a golden age of new AI labs."

## Signals
- NVIDIA's DSO and AR continue to diverge from revenue growth in subsequent quarters.
- ACIE segment growth sustains 25%+ sequential even as hyperscaler growth plateaus.
- NVIDIA equity stakes in startups grow as a substitute for cash payment.

## Counter-evidence
NVIDIA management characterized payment term extensions as applying only to "investment-grade customers," which limits the credit risk framing. DSO expansion could also reflect deal timing (large multi-quarter agreements closed late in the quarter) rather than structural financing. Single-quarter data does not confirm a trend.
