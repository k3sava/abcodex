# AI and Google serve different cognitive moments, not the same moment with different interfaces

## Claim
Matching 16,310 users across Google and AI clickstream panels, Dombrowski found that 96% of AI prompts had no corresponding Google query within 30 minutes, showing the surfaces serve separate decision points rather than competing for the same search session.

## Mechanism
AI search handles cognitive modes that involve composing, comparing, reasoning through a problem, or asking the model to do work. Google handles lookup, navigation, and quick answer retrieval. The same person uses both, but for different moments in their workflow. Because the surfaces serve distinct jobs, a shift in AI usage does not directly displace Google usage. The two are more complementary than substitutional for the same user.

## Conditions
Holds when: comparing within-session behavior for the same user across AI and traditional search. The pattern held across all 13 query types in the study.

Fails when: the query is purely factual lookup, where Google and AI handle near-identical tasks. The displacement effect is stronger in informational and research-mode queries.

## Evidence
Study: 16,310 users matched at the user level across a Google clickstream panel and an AI Prompts and Queries panel covering Perplexity, Copilot, and you.com. US desktop, November 2024. A person's Google habits explained about 4% of their AI behavior.

> "the surface matters much more" than the habit.

AI made up 2.7% of these users' total searches. The low share combined with near-zero session overlap indicates the platforms were not being substituted for each other but pulled out for different tasks.

## Signals
- Analytics show AI referral traffic is low but direct and branded search are growing. This pattern reflects presence in AI answers without session displacement.
- Users who hit AI first for a complex question then run a follow-up Google query for a specific source or product page, rather than completing the research chain inside one platform.

## Counter-evidence
The data covers November 2024 and skews toward US desktop, ages 35-44, and men. AI adoption has grown substantially since then; the overlap fraction may be higher now as AI becomes a default starting point for more query types. ChatGPT represented only 0.1% of the AI panel, so the finding may not hold for ChatGPT-dominant usage patterns. Seventy percent of Perplexity rows were truncated to 30 characters, potentially undercounting conversational prompt length.
