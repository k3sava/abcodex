# Security and governance rose from sixth to first as the enterprise AI pain point in twelve months

## Claim
Security and governance overtook context limitations as the primary enterprise AI barrier between 2025 and 2026, yet security tooling remains the largest gap between stated pain and delivered value.

## Mechanism
Context window limitations blocked AI performance in 2025 and gave teams a technical reason to stay in pilot. As context problems were solved architecturally, teams moved toward production deployment and hit a different ceiling: governance, compliance, and access control. Production deployment exposes data flows and permission boundaries that piloting never stresses. Security tooling has not kept pace with the deployment wave, leaving a gap between what teams urgently need (43% rate security as top pain point) and what they can buy (only 10% say security tooling currently delivers value).

## Conditions
Holds when: AI deployment is moving from pilot to production in regulated or enterprise environments where data handling and access-control matter. Fails when: teams remain in capability-constrained experimentation phases; then accuracy and context are still the binding constraints, not governance.

## Evidence
Theory Ventures surveyed 455 senior technical builders on September 22, 2026. Security and governance ranked first at 43% of respondents, up from sixth in the 2025 survey. Evals ranked second at 22%. Only 10% say security tooling delivers the most value today, while 18% expect it to lead value delivery in the future, the largest expected gain of any tooling category. 45% of teams route queries across multiple models.

## Signals
- Security and compliance questions appear earlier in enterprise procurement conversations.
- Teams cite access control and data residency as reasons to delay production rollout, not capability gaps.
- Security tooling vendors see faster pipeline growth than AI capability tooling vendors.

## Counter-evidence
The survey covers 455 self-selected respondents who opted in via Theory's network, not a random enterprise sample. Teams that are further along in AI adoption may overweight governance concerns compared to teams earlier in the adoption cycle.
