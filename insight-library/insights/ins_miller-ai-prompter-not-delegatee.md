# AI works better as a Socratic prompter that surfaces your thinking than as a delegatee that does the work

## Claim
Using AI to prompt you toward your own answers preserves human agency and craft; using it as a delegatee does not.

## Mechanism
Delegation to AI produces outputs the person did not think through. When AI instead asks questions, surfaces constraints, or pushes back on assumptions, the human does the reasoning and retains ownership of the result. The output may be less efficient to produce but more aligned with what the person actually knows and believes. Miller describes this as "retaining agency in craft."

## Conditions
Holds when: The work requires personal judgment, taste, or accountability for a decision. The human's own perspective is the most valuable input.
Fails when: The task is defined, well-scoped, and the human's bottleneck is execution speed rather than judgment quality.

## Evidence
Miller described his approach at Every's Thesis: 2027 conference, reported by Grant Harvey.

> "I don't believe in this world of delegating to AI as the right relationship to this technology,"

> "I want to retain the agency in my craft."

> "What if AI prompts you so you can come up with the right answer?"

## Signals
- Outputs from an AI-prompted workflow feel like yours; outputs from a delegation workflow feel like the AI's with your name on them.
- You can explain the decision in first-person without reference to what the AI said.

## Counter-evidence
Miller's position is a principled stance, not an empirical claim. For many tasks, the output quality and speed of delegation exceeds what prompting produces. The case for delegation is strongest where accountability is low and volume is high.
