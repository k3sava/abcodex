# Freshness is the single strongest predictor of listicle ranking, outweighing author signals and schema markup

## Claim
For listicle content, pages with dates from the previous two years appear in top-3 positions 66.6% of the time versus 57.3% for positions 8-10, and old or unreadable dates carry 56% lower odds of ranking in the top 3.

## Mechanism
Search engines treat freshness as a proxy for current-relevance on content types where recommendations and options change frequently. A visible old date depresses click-through rate on the SERP. Depressed CTR signals low user satisfaction, which accelerates ranking decay. The pattern is self-reinforcing: a stale page clicks less, ranks lower, receives less traffic, and loses the update motivation that would rescue it.

## Conditions
Holds when: the listicle covers a category where the best options, tools, or products change over time and users associate freshness with reliability.
Fails when: the listicle covers genuinely evergreen content where users do not associate date recency with answer quality, such as historical reference lists.

## Evidence
Indig analyzed 83 manually reviewed pages across 8 industries. Key findings: pages with dates from the previous two years appear in top-3 positions 66.6% of the time vs 57.3% for positions 8-10. Old or unreadable dates correlated with "56% lower odds of ranking in the top 3." Numbered formats ("17 Best Tools") outperformed editorial roundups in 56.8% of direct matchups, averaging position 3.77 vs 4.59. Listicles with 51-100 items in the title ranked at position 4.11 vs 5.85 for 2-5 item titles. Over-structuring with many headings correlated with 29% lower top-3 odds.

## Signals
- Updating the date on a stale, high-quality listicle produces measurable rank improvement within weeks
- Numbered-format listicles outrank editorial roundups for the same keyword
- Vendor-placed-first pages grew 3.5x faster, attributable to effort investment rather than positional bias once controls are applied

## Counter-evidence
Indig found no independent ranking lift from author bios or schema markup, contradicting a common SEO assumption. The self-promoting vendor effect disappears when other variables are controlled, suggesting effort, not favoritism, explains the growth advantage.
