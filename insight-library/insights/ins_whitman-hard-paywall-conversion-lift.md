# Hard paywalls convert five times better than freemium at Day 35 and generate eight times more revenue per install at Day 60

## Claim
Hard paywall apps (require payment or trial to access core functionality) reach a median 10.7% Day-35 trial-to-paid conversion rate. Freemium apps reach 2.1%. The same gap shows up in revenue per install at Day 60: $3.09 for hard paywall apps vs. $0.38 for freemium, an 8x difference. Despite this, apps using trials of four days or fewer increased from 42.1% to 46.5% of the total sample between 2025 and 2026.

## Mechanism
Hard paywalls create a commitment decision at the moment of highest engagement: immediately after the user discovers the product and before routine sets in. Freemium extends the evaluation period, which feels lower-friction but has two costs. First, users never face a genuine commitment moment while their interest is fresh. Second, a freemium user who has extracted value from the free tier has less motivation to pay, not more. The trial structures a deadline into the experience; that deadline makes the conversion decision unavoidable at the point of peak intent.

The 8x revenue-per-install gap at Day 60 compounds the conversion difference with a usage difference: hard paywall users who convert have demonstrated commitment through payment; their subsequent engagement tends to be higher than free users who remain on the plan indefinitely without ever having made a choice.

## Conditions
Holds when: the app's core value can be delivered meaningfully within a trial window, and the trial is structured to demonstrate the value proposition before the payment prompt. Fails when: the product requires extended use (weeks to months) before the user can evaluate its benefit. Freemium may outperform for apps where the value compounds over time and cannot be compressed into a 7-30 day window.

## Evidence
Whitman's 115,000+ app dataset (representing $16 billion in revenue) compares hard paywall and freemium apps directly on Day-35 conversion rate and Day-60 revenue per install:

- Hard paywall Day-35 conversion: 10.7%
- Freemium Day-35 conversion: 2.1%
- Hard paywall RPI at Day 60: $3.09
- Freemium RPI at Day 60: $0.38

The 46.5% share of apps using trials of four days or fewer (up from 42.1% in 2025) runs counter to the data showing shorter trials convert at 25.5% vs. 42.5% for trials of 17 to 32 days. The trend toward shorter trials persists despite the conversion evidence against it.

## Signals
- Free-tier users form habits but conversion rates stay flat despite engagement growth.
- Paywall placement comes after the user has already extracted core value.
- Conversion rate is higher in the first 24 hours post-signup than at any subsequent prompt.
- Revenue per install trails non-freemium peers in your category.

## Counter-evidence
Hard paywall conversion advantages may be category-dependent. Apps in categories where users can easily substitute (social, casual games, utilities) may see the friction of a paywall cause higher abandonment before conversion even begins, producing a lower effective conversion when the full funnel is accounted for. The benchmark numbers represent medians across all categories; high-variance categories may produce outcomes well above or below the median. Whitman does not break out conversion by category in the published summary.
