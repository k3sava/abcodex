# Calibrated expert forecasters gave AI a 2.3% chance of winning the Math Olympiad by 2025. AI won it in 2025.

## Claim
Even domain experts with strong forecasting track records have systematically underestimated near-term AI capability gains, because human intuition is calibrated for linear change while AI capabilities follow exponential curves.

## Mechanism
Human beings, including expert forecasters, calibrate predictions from prior experience with technology development rates. Nearly all prior technology improvements were incremental or at most linear over years. AI capability improvements compound year-over-year in ways that violate that intuition. An expert who correctly predicted many prior technology timelines will still be wrong on AI because the underlying growth rate does not match the pattern their intuition was trained on. The result is systematic underestimation even from people with strong domain knowledge and good general forecasting calibration.

## Conditions
Holds when: the capability domain in question improves rapidly via model scaling and training, where extrapolating recent AI performance curves gives better predictions than expert intuition.
Fails when: the specific capability domain has plateaued or is constrained by factors unrelated to compute and training (physical limits in robotics, for example); or when the forecast period is long enough that even exponential growth averages out to a pace human intuition can model.

## Evidence
In 2022, before ChatGPT's release, a group of Metaculus forecasters gave AI a 2.3% chance of winning the International Math Olympiad gold by 2025, with a median prediction of 2035. AI domain experts placed the median around 2030. In 2025, both Google and OpenAI reached gold-medal level at the Math Olympiad using large language models alone. Mollick frames this as a general pattern:

> "All the experts far underestimated the progress AI would achieve in just a few short years."

He describes AI improvement as inevitable while leaving open the question of how humans respond to it:

> "The changes AI brings are inevitable; what we do with them is not."

## Signals
- Your organization's AI roadmap extends significantly further out than what industry leaders are achieving today.
- You are discounting near-term AI capability reports because they seem implausibly fast.
- AI adoption forecasts in your planning rely on linear extrapolation of the current pace of progress.

## Counter-evidence
Not every AI capability follows an exponential curve. Robotics and physical-world AI have improved more slowly than language and reasoning tasks. Some domains may hit diminishing returns sooner. The forecasting failure is strongest where scaling compute and data has been most effective. An accurate forecaster could be right about AI in physical domains while being wrong about language and reasoning on the same timeline.
