# Attribution was designed for ad spend allocation. Its apparent usefulness for organic channels was always an illusion driven by click data.

## Claim
Attribution models were built to measure advertising budget allocation, not organic channel contribution. Clicks created a false sense of measurement confidence for organic work. Cookie deprecation and cross-device fragmentation remove the clicks, revealing a structural gap that was always there.

## Mechanism
Ad attribution assumes a closed-loop, intent-driven journey: the user sees an ad, clicks it in the same session, and converts. That structure produces a measurable causal chain. Organic discovery is non-linear. A user reads a blog post, returns three weeks later from a direct URL, and converts without ever generating a referral event traceable to the original content. Clicks gave the illusion of a causal chain for organic channels. As Indig states, attribution was invented to allocate advertising budget, never to evaluate content, SEO, or community. The measurement gap for organic was always present. Technology changes are making it visible.

## Conditions
Holds when: a meaningful share of the channel mix includes organic search, brand content, thought leadership, events, or community, where discovery and conversion are non-linear and multi-session.

Fails when: the business runs almost entirely on paid direct-response campaigns where the click-to-convert path is genuinely short and attribution accuracy is high.

## Evidence
Indig published "The Collapse of Attribution" on September 28, 2026, drawing on a conversation with George Bonaci, VP Growth at Ramp. Bonaci characterizes the practical consequence: attribution has become "a crutch replacing critical thinking." The argument is that marketers anchored to attribution data stop reasoning about channel contribution and start chasing the metrics that register in the dashboard.

## Signals
- Organic investment stagnates while paid scales, reflecting attribution bias toward channels that produce trackable conversions.
- Teams reduce SEO, content, or community spend after attribution dashboards fail to credit it, despite sustained direct traffic growth.
- Marketing headcount concentrates in paid while organic functions shrink, a structural version of the same attribution bias.

## Counter-evidence
Attribution skeptics risk the opposite error: funding channels with no evidence of contribution at all. A framework that replaces attribution entirely can justify any spend. The argument against attribution needs to propose a replacement measurement approach, not just an absence of measurement. Indig acknowledges this and proposes triangulation as the successor framework.
