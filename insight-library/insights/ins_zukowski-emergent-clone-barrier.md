# Games built on emergent, interacting systems resist cloning because their core appeal cannot be evaluated from screenshots or descriptions

## Claim
Games whose value emerges from unexpected interactions among deep systems create a comprehension barrier that defeats cloners, because the game's appeal is only perceptible through extended play, not through observation from a distance.

## Mechanism
Cloners reverse-engineer a game's value proposition from its visible presentation: screenshots, trailers, descriptions, and player reactions. Games built on a single visible gimmick expose their entire appeal on first sight, giving cloners everything they need to replicate it. Games built on emergent systems hide their appeal inside the act of playing. The interactions that make the experience compelling cannot be inferred from screenshots or described accurately in text. This comprehension barrier is a natural moat: a cloner cannot replicate what they cannot understand, and they cannot understand it without playing deeply. AI cloning compounds this: AI tools scrape visible presentation signals, not felt experience.

## Conditions
Holds when: the game's appeal requires extended play to perceive; the interactions are genuinely emergent, not just complex. Fails when: the emergent systems can be explained in a compelling one-sentence pitch (which also serves as a brief for cloners); or when the audience is small enough that the comprehension barrier also stops the target market from discovering the game at all.

## Evidence
Zukowski identifies four features that increase clone vulnerability, and emergence is the natural inverse of each:

> "A cloner can't clone what can't be experienced until you play the game."

His four vulnerability signals: gimmick-driven concepts (the full appeal is in one mechanic, visible on first sight), non-distinctive art (easy to imitate), well-established genre conventions (easy to mix and match), and systematic content with a simple end state (meta numbers and clear goals are replicable from screenshots). A game that inverts all four signals is as clone-resistant as a game can be.

## Signals
- Press coverage defaults to describing mechanics rather than explaining the game's feel.
- Streams and let's plays convert viewers to buyers at a higher rate than trailers.
- Clones appear on Steam but ship with a noticeably different feel despite similar mechanics and screenshots.

## Counter-evidence
Emergence also resists discoverability. A game whose appeal can't be conveyed visually is also harder to market to its own audience. Zukowski acknowledges this tradeoff implicitly: clone-resistant games often find smaller initial audiences. There is no data in the post quantifying how often emergent mechanics produce chart-topping games versus durable niche hits.
