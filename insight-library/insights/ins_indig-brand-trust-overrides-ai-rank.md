# In AI search, trusted brands win regardless of recommendation position; rank is irrelevant when a known brand appears anywhere on the shortlist

## Claim
Seventy-five percent of users default to the AI's top-ranked recommendation, but when a trusted brand appears anywhere on an AI shortlist, users select it over the top-ranked option at an 88% rate. Position optimization is the wrong lever; brand trust is the actual conversion mechanism.

## Mechanism
AI search surfaces a shortlist of candidates, not a single answer. Users scan that shortlist using memory, not logic. A brand name that already exists in the user's trust register triggers selection even from the third or fourth position. The model's recommendation rank shapes behavior only when the user has no existing brand association for any option. The implication Indig draws: the unit of optimization in AI search is whether AI names, trusts, and recommends your brand, not whether your content ranks first in any one engine.

## Conditions
Holds when: the user is choosing among alternatives and at least one option is a familiar brand. The brand recognition advantage is strongest in categories with established market leaders where users have prior purchase or evaluation experience.

Fails when: all options are unfamiliar (pure commodity queries), or when the task is informational rather than a buying decision. Also fails in categories where brand awareness is low across all options, such as new product categories without established players.

## Evidence
Indig's H1 2026 report analyzed consumer behavior data across AI search sessions:

> "Close to 75% of consumers pick the number one result in an AI shortlist. But if they see a trusted brand anywhere on that list, they will pick it."

The second figure, 88% selecting trusted brands regardless of position, comes from AI Mode product recommendation data where high-intent buying sessions were tracked. Indig's framework for this: AEO and GEO are brand marketing channels in performance marketing clothing. The metrics that matter are brand mentions, recommendation frequency, and sentiment in AI answers, not citation count or rank position.

## Signals
- A competitor brand with lower AI citation rate closes more deals from AI-referred traffic than you do.
- Your brand's conversion rate from AI-referred sessions is lower than from branded search, suggesting low AI-context brand recognition.
- A/B testing ad spend on brand awareness in non-search channels correlates with higher AI shortlist selection rates.

## Counter-evidence
The 88% figure covers AI Mode product recommendations, a high-intent buying surface. Lower-intent queries (informational, exploratory) likely show weaker brand trust override effects. For commodity categories where price is the primary axis, brand may not override position. The data is from H1 2026, when AI search is still early; user behavior may shift as AI recommendation UI matures and users develop new trust patterns for AI-curated lists.
