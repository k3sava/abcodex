# When every brand in a content category declines together in AI search, the platform is repricing the category, not re-ranking competitors

## Claim
Simultaneous AI visibility decline across all brands in a vertical signals that the AI platform is reducing demand for the entire content category. The correct response is a pivot, not better positioning within the category.

## Mechanism
Individual site ranking losses redistribute share: one brand goes down as another rises. Category-wide synchronized decline works differently. The AI surface finds an in-place substitute for the category's core function and stops routing users to any external brand. For stock photos, generative image tools satisfy the lookup in-place. No brand captures the traffic that previously went to all of them combined, because the AI surface no longer sends users out of the platform for that category at all.

## Conditions
Holds when: all named brands in a vertical decline within the same measurement window; no single brand shows positive movement absorbing lost share; the content category has a credible AI-generated substitute.
Fails when: only some brands decline while one or more gain; the decline reverses within two measurement periods; the category provides specialized expert content with no AI substitute.

## Evidence
From Indig's Growth Intelligence Brief #22 (August 6, 2026), covering AI search visibility for the 30 days ending July 15, 2026: all 9 named stock photo and photo-print brands declined in the same period, ranging from -8.7% to -44.7%. Indig's direct interpretation: "A decline this broad and synchronized reads as category repricing rather than nine separate site problems."

## Signals
- Every direct competitor in the vertical shows negative AI visibility in the same reporting window
- No competitor absorbs a share gain proportional to others' losses
- The category provides a service that AI generation can now satisfy without an external referral

## Counter-evidence
A 30-day measurement window can capture timing noise rather than a structural shift. Indig's data covers primarily commercial verticals; the repricing pattern may not appear in content categories without a credible AI substitute. AI Mode's overall 12.9% contraction in this window may amplify declines that would look smaller in a period of stable AI surface usage.
