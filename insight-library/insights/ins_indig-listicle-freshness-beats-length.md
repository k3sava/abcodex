# Listicle ranking is driven by freshness and explicit enumeration, not content length; dated pages with visible recency signals and counted lists beat editorial roundups

## Claim
The two strongest predictors of listicle ranking are visible date recency and explicit enumeration. Long editorial roundups without counted titles and fresh dates are outranked by shorter, clearly numbered pages with visible publication dates from the last two years.

## Mechanism
Freshness signals recency and relevance to ranking systems. A visible date tells both algorithms and users when the page was last confirmed accurate. Stale dates suppress ranking independent of content quality: Indig's data shows a 56% lower odds of top-3 ranking for pages with outdated dates.

Explicit enumeration reduces ambiguity. A title that says "37 tools" commits to a count; readers and ranking systems can verify and anchor expectations. Non-numbered roundups ("The Best Tools for X") leave quantity ambiguous. In direct matchups between counted and non-counted listicles, counted pages took better positions in 56.8% of cases.

Excessive heading structure adds navigation overhead that correlates with lower rankings. More headings increase page complexity without increasing ranking clarity. This may reflect user behavior (pages with many headings signal longer content, correlating with lower engagement rates) or algorithmic preference for tighter signal density.

## Conditions
Holds when: the query is listicle-format (tool roundups, ranked lists, category comparisons). The 83-page analysis spans 8 industries, suggesting the pattern is query-type general rather than niche-specific.

Fails when: the query requires a definitive authority rather than a current list, or when freshness is irrelevant (evergreen how-to content, reference documentation). A long, deeply sourced listicle with a stale date can be rescued by a visible update timestamp.

## Evidence
Indig manually reviewed 83 pages across 8 industries. Freshness data: pages dated within the previous 2 years appeared in top-3 positions at 66.6% vs. 57.3% for positions 8-10. Outdated date pages had 56% lower odds of reaching top-3.

Enumeration data: counted listicles beat non-numbered roundups in 56.8% of direct matchups (mean position 3.77 vs. 4.59). Titles with 51-100 items showed the best mean rank (4.11) compared to titles with 2-5 items (5.85 mean rank).

Heading structure data: one standard deviation increase in heading count produced 29% lower top-3 odds.

## Signals
- An existing listicle ranking at positions 4-8 with a date older than 2 years is a candidate for date refresh before content expansion.
- Titles without explicit counts underperform equivalent pages with numbered counts in direct ranking comparisons.
- Heading reduction on overbuilt pages may improve ranking performance without content changes.

## Counter-evidence
The 83-page manual review is a small sample and covers only the listicle format. The heading-count correlation may be confounded by content domain (technical reference content naturally uses more headings). The freshness signal advantage may weaken when a topic is stable enough that ranking systems discount date as a relevance signal.
