# Above $1T enterprise value, earnings multiples cap revenue multiples regardless of growth rate

## Claim
Once a company's enterprise value passes roughly $1 trillion, public markets price it on a P/E basis rather than on revenue growth. Because a revenue multiple equals P/E multiple times net margin, the earnings anchor caps how high the revenue multiple can go no matter how fast the company grows.

## Mechanism
Trillion-dollar companies trade in a narrow 20x to 26x P/E band. That range reflects the market's demand for earnings certainty at that scale. Net margin is fixed by the business model. The product of the two sets a ceiling: Microsoft at 41% net margin and 26x P/E caps at approximately 10x forward sales. Meta and Google at roughly 25% margin and 20x P/E cap near 5x. A company growing 466% and one growing 20% will trade at the same forward revenue multiple if they have the same market cap and margin profile. Growth above a threshold contributes nothing to multiple expansion because the market has already shifted to pricing terminal earnings.

## Conditions
Holds when: The company has crossed approximately $1T enterprise value and comparable public-market or late-stage private investors are setting the price.
Fails when: The company has negligible current earnings and investors are pricing on a distant terminal value at an earlier stage where revenue multiples dominate valuation conversations.

## Evidence
Tunguz analyzed Anthropic ($1.56T market cap, 9.5x 2027 EV/revenue, implied growth 288%) and SpaceX ($1.82T, 9.5x 2027 EV/revenue, implied growth 466%). Both converged on the same forward revenue multiple despite being completely different businesses with radically different growth trajectories. Among all trillion-dollar companies, none trades significantly above 10x forward sales.

> "Above $1t of enterprise value, public markets stop paying for top-line growth"

> "multiple expansion is over"

## Signals
- Two companies with very different growth rates trade at similar forward revenue multiples once both pass $1T.
- Late-stage investors in a next round cite P/E comparables rather than revenue comps.
- Internal growth projections stop moving the needle in valuation conversations.

## Counter-evidence
Companies with no current earnings and an extreme monopoly position could in principle trade above the P/E wall if investors price on a distant terminal value. The pattern also breaks in market dislocations where investors temporarily abandon earnings discipline. The sample of trillion-dollar private companies is small; the data point is Anthropic and SpaceX only.
