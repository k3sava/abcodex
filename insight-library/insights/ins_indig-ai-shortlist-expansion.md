# AI expands B2B software shortlists rather than narrowing them: 63% of buyers add a vendor mid-conversation

## Claim
AI does not narrow a software buyer's shortlist. It reopens it. Among 200 SaaS buyers who already had candidates, 63% added another vendor through AI, because the conversational format lets buyers test and refine criteria in real time.

## Mechanism
Traditional search is stateless. A buyer runs a query, picks links, and builds a shortlist from outside the search engine. AI search is stateful and conversational. When an initial answer misses a buyer's criteria, they add a constraint ("eliminate any vendors without a Marketo API") or push back on the framing ("I care about product-level insights, not just brand level"). The dialogue loop runs until the shortlist fits. This keeps the pool open longer than a one-shot query. Getting into the first answer matters, but it does not lock in a win. Every follow-up is a new gate, and each gate tests pricing, integrations, fit, and setup. A vendor that passes the first screen can still be eliminated in round three. A vendor that missed the first screen can enter at round two.

## Conditions
Holds when: buyers are evaluating a category they have moderate familiarity with and use AI to accelerate comparison; the category has enough differentiation on specific criteria (integrations, pricing tiers, vertical fit) that follow-up questions meaningfully filter the field.
Fails when: buyers already have strong brand loyalty and use AI only for confirmation; the buying decision is so narrow that the initial AI answer is the only answer needed; enterprise procurement processes bypass the exploratory phase and go straight to RFP.

## Evidence
Wynter survey of 200 SaaS buyers (self-reported behavior). Key figures:
- 99 of 158 buyers with existing candidates (62.7%) added a vendor through AI.
- 78 of 126 final-decision buyers said the chosen vendor appeared in the first AI answer. Of those 78, 75 still sent follow-up messages.
- Median follow-ups per session: 3-5. About a third sent 6 or more.
- Most common follow-up topics: pricing (57.5%), vendor comparisons (54.5%), integrations (46%), setup (40%).
- After the AI conversation: 72.5% visited vendor websites, 48% also searched Google.
- 67.5% used more than one AI platform in their research.

Buyer quotes Indig cites:

> "I care about product level insights, not just brand level."

> "Eliminate any vendors that don't have an API integration with Marketo."

Limitations: The sample is 200 self-selected respondents via Wynter's panel. Purchase outcomes were not independently verified. The survey cannot distinguish AI-caused vendor additions from additions that would have happened anyway.

## Signals
- Brand appears in AI search citations for multi-turn conversational queries, not just head-term answers.
- Pricing and integration pages receive traffic from AI-referred sessions, suggesting buyers verified those criteria after an AI conversation.
- Pipeline includes vendors who appeared late in a buyer's research, not just those with high initial brand awareness.

## Counter-evidence
Indig's existing cards on AI Mode in e-commerce (ins_indig-ai-mode-shortlist-acceptance) show the opposite dynamic: AI Mode collapses product discovery to a pre-curated shortlist that 88% of e-commerce users accept without checking. The expansion effect may be specific to high-consideration B2B software buying, where criteria complexity is high and the cost of a wrong choice justifies more follow-up rounds. E-commerce buyers with lower commitment may accept the first AI answer. The Wynter survey sample may also overrepresent tech-savvy buyers who use AI more actively than the median enterprise buyer.
