# Screen for prior ambiguous situations, not qualifications, to predict AI-era performance

## Claim
In an AI-accelerated environment where tasks are ambiguous and change fast, the best hiring signal is whether a candidate has survived genuinely ambiguous situations before, not what they know or what they have shipped.

## Mechanism
AI handles well-defined tasks; the remaining human work is judgment in ambiguity. That tolerance for ambiguity is a property of experience, not of credentials. Anderson's interview approach extracts the evidence directly: asking candidates to describe near-death professional experiences surfaces whether they have that history. Someone who has not had the experience cannot fake it, and someone who has will have concrete stories.

## Conditions
Holds when: The role involves undefined problems, rapid iteration, and situations where no procedure exists yet.
Fails when: The role is well-defined and procedural; a history of surviving ambiguity may predict restlessness or discomfort in structured environments.

## Evidence
Anderson described his interview practice at Every's Thesis: 2027 conference, reported by Grant Harvey.

> "where you've been in an ambiguous situation and had to survive or grow through it."

> "Tell me how many near-death experiences you've had."

> "Do you have tolerance for uncertainty?"

He also uses iteration as a proxy: "so much of life is about iterations. You just need at-bats."

## Signals
- Candidates who describe a moment of near-failure and what they did to get through it; those who give polished narratives about past challenges that resolved neatly may not have the history Anderson is looking for.
- Teams built this way resolve ambiguous problems faster without escalating for guidance.

## Counter-evidence
Self-selection problem: near-death professional experiences correlate with risk-taking, which is not always productive. It may also overindex toward founders and people who chose high-variance paths, underweighting excellent operators who prefer well-defined environments.
