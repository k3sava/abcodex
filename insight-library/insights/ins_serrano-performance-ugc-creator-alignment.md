# Paying UGC creators a share of ad spend, not a flat rate, selects for authentic advocates and aligns creator economics with purchase outcomes

## Claim
Performance-based creator compensation, where creators receive a percentage of ad spend rather than a flat appearance fee, filters out mercenary contributors and surfaces the creators who genuinely use and believe in the product. Those are the same creators whose content performs at scale.

## Mechanism
Flat-rate UGC payments decouple creator incentive from campaign outcome. A creator paid $500 per video earns the same whether the video drives $5,000 or $50,000 in attributed revenue. Under a revenue-share model, the creator only earns well if the content works. Authenticity functions as a performance predictor: creators who actually use the product produce content that converts because their reactions are real. The compensation structure makes the selection self-enforcing. Creators who want stable income at $13k+/month have to produce work that drives purchases, which requires genuine product affinity.

## Conditions
Holds when: the product has measurable attribution per creative (tracked ad spend); the creator base is large enough to test at volume (Wispr Flow runs 50 creators producing 500 to 600 ads per month); the product category is one where authentic user experience provides credible social proof.
Fails when: attribution is too noisy to tie a creator's content to revenue; the product is not one people use daily and visibly; creators can game the metric through non-converting clicks.

## Evidence
Hector Serrano described Wispr Flow's UGC approach in a November 2026 Lenny's Podcast episode. The company runs a 50-creator roster producing 500 to 600 paid ads per month. Top-performing creators earn over $13,000 per month. Serrano noted that the best creators are what he called "allergic to AI," meaning they avoid AI-generated content in their own work, which is what makes them credible voices for an AI product. Wispr Flow reported roughly 30x revenue growth in the year preceding the interview.

## Signals
- Creators on the roster renew and increase output month over month without renegotiation
- Top-of-funnel creative volume is high while cost-per-acquisition stays below threshold
- The same five to ten creators account for a disproportionate share of attributed revenue

## Counter-evidence
Performance-based models shift financial risk to creators, which makes them harder to recruit and retain at the start of a program when attribution data does not yet exist. Early-stage brands with no attribution infrastructure cannot run this model. Flat-rate payments may be necessary to build a roster before switching to performance share.
