# The emerging high-value role is teaching AI to do your work and continuously raising the bar

## Claim
As AI takes over execution, the durable human role shifts from doing to teaching: specifying, correcting, and raising the standard that AI must meet.

## Mechanism
AI systems improve when given feedback on what good looks like in a specific context. The person closest to the work is best positioned to give that feedback. Over time this loop raises the AI's capability for that work faster than any generic training can. The skill this rewards is not execution but the ability to articulate standards and act as a discerning evaluator.

## Conditions
Holds when: The work is complex enough that "good" is contextual and requires judgment to define; the AI can learn from corrections.
Fails when: The task has objective right answers; there is no feedback loop available to the person doing the work.

## Evidence
George described this shift at Every's Thesis: 2027 conference, reported by Grant Harvey.

> "not doing stuff ourselves, but actually teaching AI to do it,"

> "continually and constantly raising the bar."

## Signals
- Practitioners spend more time reviewing and correcting AI outputs than producing their own.
- The highest-value people on a team are those who can articulate what a good output looks like, not those who produce the most outputs.

## Counter-evidence
Teaching AI to do your work is itself a form of labor that may not transfer across roles or systems. If the AI changes models or the system resets, the teaching investment may not carry over. Organizations may also find that "raising the bar" bottlenecks on the teacher's time, limiting how much AI-accelerated work can happen in parallel.
