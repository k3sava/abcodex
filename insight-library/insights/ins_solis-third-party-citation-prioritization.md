# Six criteria for deciding which third-party platforms are worth investing in for AI search citations

## Claim
Because AI citation sources vary by vertical, platform, and model release, a six-criterion framework helps marketers prioritize which third-party environments to invest in: citation influence, brand association potential, marketing alignment, business value, durability under platform change, and expected effort.

## Mechanism
The prior finding that 69-82% of AI citations come from external domains does not tell a marketing team where to spend its budget. AI platforms reshuffle citation sources as models update, and not every third-party environment is equally worth investing in. A one-size-fits-all approach wastes resources on platforms with low citation influence or poor alignment with business goals.

The six criteria make prioritization explicit. Citation influence measures how strongly a format or platform affects AI inclusion and recommendation likelihood. Brand association potential measures whether the platform connects the brand to relevant categories and problems. Marketing alignment asks whether the investment supports existing PR or community programs rather than requiring a parallel effort. Business value ties each activity to acquisition, conversion, retention, or expansion goals. Durability under platform change asks whether the investment retains value if AI models update their citation weighting. Expected effort accounts for editorial, technical, and maintenance resources required.

Scoring each candidate investment against all six criteria surfaces which activities have high impact across multiple dimensions and which are narrow wins (high citation influence, low business value) that can be deprioritized.

## Conditions
Holds when: a brand has validated that AI search contributes to its discovery funnel; budget for third-party investment is constrained and choices must be made; AI citation sources in the brand's vertical are shifting.

Fails when: a brand has no existing AI search presence and needs to establish a minimum viable footprint first (in which case identifying citation sources is the prior step, not prioritizing among them). Fails when all six dimensions score identically across options (rare in practice).

## Evidence
Solis frames the core problem the framework addresses:

> "Which third-party citations and platforms are actually worth investing in when the sources used by AI search platforms can change by vertical, prompt, market, platform and model release?"

This piece extends her August 2 research (external domains account for 69-82% of AI citations) from diagnosis to decision-making, providing a worksheet that scores each third-party investment against the six dimensions.

## Signals
- A marketing team is debating Reddit presence vs. industry publication placement vs. community partnerships; the framework resolves the debate by scoring each option against all six criteria
- An activity scoring high on citation influence but low on business value is deprioritized in favor of one that scores high on both
- Durability under platform change penalizes platform-specific tactics that lose value when AI models update their citation sources

## Counter-evidence
The framework does not account for competitive dynamics: investing in a platform only has value if competitors do not already dominate there. The six criteria are qualitative; scoring requires judgment and may produce inconsistent results across team members without a shared calibration session.
