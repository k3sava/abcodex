# AI search category ownership concentrates fast and holds: owners win on brand mentions, not citations, and retain first position 90 percent of the time

## Claim
Analysis of 50,000+ brands shows that category owners in AI search are defined by brand mention dominance rather than citation frequency, hold first position in 90.4% of month-over-month comparisons, and command 55.7% higher brand search volume than runners-up. The majority of AI search demand, 89.3% by Indig's estimate, sits in categories with no clear owner yet.

## Mechanism
AI search engines cite brands whose names appear consistently across the web corpora they index and the training data they learn from. Brand mentions (how often a brand is referenced by others across the web) predict AI citation share more reliably than reverse-engineered citation metrics, because mentions reflect the breadth and consistency of recognition that AI models use to form a "default answer" for category queries.

Once a brand achieves category ownership in AI search, the position self-reinforces: citations drive brand awareness, brand awareness drives web mentions, web mentions feed back into training data and retrieval corpora. This flywheel makes wide-margin leadership highly durable (category leaders retained position 90.4% of months). Narrow-margin leads switch more often (median 1.3-point advantage in switching categories vs. wide-margin stability), which means the strategic priority is not just achieving ownership but achieving a durable margin.

The important implication of the citation-mention relationship being slightly negative (-0.229): brands that chase citations without building genuine brand authority do not convert to ownership. The metric to optimize is brand mentions in AI-generated responses, not the count of backlinks or structured data citations that traditional SEO optimized.

## Conditions
Holds when: the category is well-defined enough for AI to form a consistent "category owner" answer; the brand has operated in the space long enough to accumulate web mentions; the AI engines queried are the main search surfaces for the category.

Fails when: the category is so broad that AI engines fragment the answer across many brands rather than picking a leader; a new entrant with significantly higher brand recognition in adjacent categories reframes the category definition; the category is too niche to have trained a strong prior in AI search corpora.

## Evidence
Kevin Indig, analyzing 50,000+ brands across AI search engines, published July 20, 2026:

- 15.2% of categories have clear owners.
- 53.7% remain open fields with multiple contenders.
- 89.3% of estimated AI search demand sits in categories with no clear owner.
- Category owners: 55.7% higher brand search volume, 48.4% higher organic traffic, 52.5% higher authority scores than runners-up.
- Retention: 90.4% month-over-month first-place retention for category owners.
- Citation-to-mention correlation: slightly negative (-0.229), meaning optimizing citations without building brand mentions does not create ownership.

## Signals
- Brand mention frequency in AI-generated responses increases each quarter without a corresponding increase in traditional SEO rankings.
- Brand search volume grows faster than organic traffic growth, signaling AI search driving brand discovery.
- Competitors mention your brand name in AI-generated responses when users ask about the category.

## Counter-evidence
The 53.7% of open categories means most brands are not facing an entrenched owner yet, and a strong campaign can establish ownership without historical advantage. Brand mentions correlating with ownership means high brand-ad spend can establish AI search position without content depth, which narrows the window for content-led SEO strategies to claim ownership against well-funded competitors. The analysis is also a snapshot: category ownership may be more fragile as AI model updates change which sources they weight.
