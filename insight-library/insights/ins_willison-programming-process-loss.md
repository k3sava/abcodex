# Whether AI agents help or hurt programmers depends on what they valued about coding in the first place

## Claim
The AI-agent takeover of programming is a compounding gift for programmers who valued working software above the solving process, and a real loss for those who valued the puzzles themselves.

## Mechanism
Programming historically delivered two types of reward. Outcome reward: working software ships. Process reward: the puzzle-solving itself. Agents automate the process steps. For outcome-focused programmers, agents multiply throughput with no loss. For process-focused programmers, agents remove the primary source of satisfaction. The same capability produces opposite effects depending on the programmer's intrinsic motivation structure.

Willison draws on Jeremy Scheff's framing: programmers once "got paid to work on tricky little logic puzzles" and "set the strategy, defined what was and was not possible." Agents now do the puzzle work. Willison adds the split: those for whom the output mattered gain, those for whom the solving mattered lose.

## Conditions
Holds when: AI agents reliably complete the problem-solving steps at a level that removes the need for human judgment; the programmer actively chooses to use them.
Fails when: the problems sit at the frontier where AI cannot yet substitute; process and outcome reward become inseparable because the puzzle cannot be delegated yet.

## Evidence
Willison's October 2026 link post adds his own framing to Jeremy Scheff's "My obituary for programming":

> "LLMs are the gift that keeps on giving" [for those who "don't care too deeply about the problems solved along the way."]

> "If directly solving those problems was a big part of your enjoyment, the loss is very real."

## Signals
- Teams adopting agentic coding see high voluntary adoption among outcome-oriented engineers and disengagement from process-oriented ones doing the same tasks.
- Post-project retrospectives on agentic work surface divergent satisfaction scores across engineers with similar outputs.

## Counter-evidence
Process-focused programmers often find new process reward at the conjurer level: system design, output review, deciding when to intervene. The loss may be transitional rather than permanent, as new craft patterns emerge at higher abstraction levels. Some report that agentic coding surfaces harder problems faster, increasing the density of genuinely interesting puzzle work.
