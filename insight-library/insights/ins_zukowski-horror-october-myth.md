# October is the third-worst revenue month for indie horror games because Steam algorithm amplification is diluted by a 60 percent surge in competing horror releases

## Claim
October underperforms June for indie horror games by median revenue. The seasonal association that fills October with horror releases creates competitive oversaturation without a proportional increase in buyer demand, diluting each title's algorithmic amplification.

## Mechanism
Steam's recommendation engine rewards recent sales velocity and wishlist-to-purchase conversion rate. In October, roughly 60 percent more horror games launch than in June, splitting attention across a buyer pool that does not grow proportionally with developer supply. Each game gets a smaller share of available algorithmic surface area. In June, the same buyer interest is spread across far fewer titles, so each launch benefits from higher algorithmic lift per unit of buyer demand. Seasonal games festivals (Steam Scream Fest, Haunted House Festival) favor titles with existing sales momentum, not launch-day entrants.

## Conditions
Holds when: the horror game is an indie title competing for algorithmic discoverability rather than riding pre-existing community demand. The pattern applies to the majority of the horror catalog, where no title has enough pre-launch wishlist mass to create its own traffic.

Fails when: a title has a large enough wishlist base to guarantee launch-day sales velocity regardless of competition. The two strongest horror titles in Zukowski's analysis (Lethal Company, No More Room in Hell) launched in October, but they represent the exception at the top of the distribution. Publishers with established catalogs (New Blood Interactive, DreadXP) also use October more heavily because they have existing community demand that offsets competitive dilution.

## Evidence
Zukowski analyzed 7,019 horror games on Steam and found October ranks third-worst by median revenue for what he calls "serious" horror developers. June shows the highest median revenue despite having no horror-specific festival. Of the nine top-performing horror titles in his dataset, only two launched in October.

> "The first rule of becoming a professional Steam developer is that you must realize your gut instinct is wrong"

The seasonal movie analogy fails for games: horror movies run 2 hours and game switching costs are high. Players do not rotate games seasonally the way audiences switch from summer blockbusters to Halloween releases.

## Signals
- Wishlist conversion rate at launch holds or improves vs. comparable June launches
- Scream Fest coverage correlates with pre-existing sales momentum, not freshness
- Genre search traffic does not spike in October at the same rate as genre supply

## Counter-evidence
The top two horror games by revenue (Lethal Company, No More Room in Hell) both launched in October, which complicates the aggregate picture. The data supports the claim at the median level, not at the extreme right tail. A game with exceptional word-of-mouth potential may benefit from the cultural moment regardless of supply dynamics. Publishers with established audience relationships also see different dynamics from the single-game indie launching cold.
