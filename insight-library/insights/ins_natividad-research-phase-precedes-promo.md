# For high-consideration purchases, the research phase decides conversion before the promotional window opens

## Claim
For purchases above $100 that require buyer justification, brand citations in research environments (editorial, clinical, community, AI chatbots) during the 60-90 days before a promotional window determine conversion outcomes more than the promotional window itself. SparkToro audience data for LED anti-aging device buyers shows 221% over-indexing on Claude relative to average web users and under-indexing across all major social platforms.

## Mechanism
High-consideration purchases have a two-stage decision: "is this category credible?" and "is this brand the right choice?" Both questions get answered during a research phase that precedes any purchase intent. When the buyer is ready to convert, the brand either exists in their trusted reference set or it does not. Promotional offers during the buying window (Black Friday, for example) accelerate decisions that were pre-made during research.

The implication for timing is specific. A brand that waits until October to build awareness for a November promotional window has missed the research phase. The audience has already formed category and brand credibility assessments.

AI chatbots have become a primary research surface for considered purchases. An audience that over-indexes on Claude by 221% is not using AI for casual browsing. It is using AI to research and evaluate. A brand that does not appear in AI search results during the research phase is invisible at the moment of pre-decision.

The four-phase sequence Natividad describes: win the research phase in August-September (editorial placement, clinical citations, community presence, AI citation); borrow trust in September-October (influencer alignment, third-party validation); facilitate comparison in October-November (review aggregators, head-to-head content); execute promotions in November. Each phase depends on the prior.

## Conditions
Holds when: the purchase requires 60+ days of research before conversion, involves meaningful financial commitment, and sits in a category where buyer trust is established through sources outside the brand's owned channels.

Fails when: purchase decisions are impulsive (low price, low risk), or when the brand already saturates the research environment.

## Evidence
SparkToro audience intelligence on women seeking LED anti-aging treatments: 221% over-index on Claude usage relative to average web users. YouTube: 8% below average. TikTok: 13% below average. Top consideration queries in organic search: "led face mask reviews," "led face mask before and after," "does led face mask work."

High-affinity research sources include Allure, NewBeauty, the American Academy of Dermatology, dermatologist content, and r/SkincareAddiction (99/100 affinity score). The audience does not research on social. It researches on clinical and editorial sources, and increasingly on AI assistants.

## Signals
- Brand appears in AI chatbot responses when users search category consideration queries.
- Editorial and clinical citations grow in the 60-90 day window before a promotional season.
- Organic traffic from consideration-stage queries increases before brand-name or conversion queries.

## Counter-evidence
The case is drawn from a single product category (LED skincare) and a single promotional window (Black Friday). Different categories may have shorter research phases or higher social-platform affinity. The Claude over-index data reflects audience platform usage broadly, not necessarily purchase research intent specifically.
