# AI agents remove the switching friction that protected incumbent revenue from customers who never got around to leaving

## Claim
Businesses that relied on customer inattention and switching cost to retain revenue are now exposed, because AI agents can automate the comparison and switching process that those customers never got around to doing.

## Mechanism
Traditional businesses in sectors like telecommunications, banking, and insurance built pricing structures that extracted value from customers who would not spend time comparing alternatives or switching providers. That inertia was not a permanent customer trait but a rational response to friction. The comparison was time-consuming, the switching process was confusing, and the perceived gain was not worth the effort. AI agents remove the friction: they can compare plans, identify overpayments, and initiate switching on behalf of users. When the agent does the work the customer would not, the switching cost evaporates and so does the revenue built on it.

Evans calls this "inertia is no longer a moat." He frames the timeline as years, not quarters. The initial evidence is from early-adopter agent use cases, not mass deployment, and most consumer agents are still nascent. But the structural premise is already visible: the agent removes the information asymmetry and the effort cost that the incumbent relied on.

## Conditions
Holds when: the customer's reluctance to switch was driven by time, complexity, or attention cost rather than genuine switching costs (e.g., data portability, vendor-specific integrations, contractual lock-in). The asset being defended is a pricing premium over a commodity offering.
Fails when: switching genuinely requires migration effort the agent cannot do (platform dependencies, custom integrations, training time). Also fails when the customer's inertia is relationship-based rather than friction-based.

## Evidence
From Evans' November 18, 2026 AI Questions presentation, as reported by i-SCOOP:

Goldman Sachs estimates 5% to 20% of free cash flow for UK mobile operators is at risk if out-of-contract customers use AI agents to optimize plans.

Apollo's Torsten Slok raised the possibility of an "agentic bank run," with assistants moving cash from accounts earning 0.1% to accounts earning 3.3 to 5%.

A health insurance study documents people overpaying by thousands of dollars, mostly through inattention and switching hassle. That cost disappears once an agent monitors the market on their behalf.

Uber reported about 500 orders and 4 rides from third-party agents during Halloween week, out of roughly 320 million trips. Evans cited this as early-stage evidence, not a current threat.

## Signals
- Competitor or newcomer advertising agents as comparison or switching tools in your category.
- An increase in plan optimization requests from customers using third-party tools.
- Rising churn or inquiry volume that traces to a specific AI-powered comparison product.
- Regulatory scrutiny of loyalty pricing structures in your sector, prompted by agent-enabled transparency.

## Counter-evidence
The evidence is early. Uber's Halloween data is a rounding error. Mass agent-driven switching requires consumers to trust agents with sensitive accounts, which is a behavioral hurdle that may slow the timeline significantly. Some incumbents will use the same agents to retain customers by preemptively offering better deals, turning the agent into a retention tool rather than only a disruption threat.
