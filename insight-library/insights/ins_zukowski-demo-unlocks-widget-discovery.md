# A free demo gives a game access to Steam's discovery widgets, which generated 48 times more wishlists than YouTube Shorts

## Claim
A free demo qualifies a game for Steam's New & Trending Free discovery widget, which generates far more wishlists than social media campaigns. One game earned 36,946 wishlists from the widget versus 769 from YouTube Shorts for the same title.

## Mechanism
Steam's wishlist engine runs primarily inside the platform, not outside it. Players browsing Steam are in a discovery mindset; viewers watching YouTube Shorts are not. The New & Trending Free widget has high placement and high traffic from players actively looking for their next game. A game with a free demo qualifies for that widget and the broader set of discovery surfaces: the Discovery Queue, content creator visibility programs, and Steam Next Fest eligibility.

Social media generates external impressions, but most impressions do not convert because viewers are not at the point in their day where they are ready to wishlist a game. Steam widget impressions convert at a fundamentally different rate because the friction between seeing the game and adding it to a wishlist is near zero inside the platform.

Zukowski documented the gap directly using "How Many Dudes?" During the Steam Winter Sale period, the game spent at least 24 days on the front-page New & Trending Free widget through its demo. That widget generated 36,946 wishlists. YouTube Shorts campaigns for the same game generated 769 wishlists over a comparable period. The ratio is approximately 48 to 1 in the algorithm's favor.

## Conditions
Holds when: the demo has genuine resonance (median playtime above 60 minutes; players want to continue after finishing); the demo is released well ahead of the full launch so widget exposure can compound.

Fails when: the demo itself is low quality (low median playtime, players abandon early); the demo is launched too close to the full game release for the widget exposure to build wishlist count; the game is in a genre with low algorithmic affinity on Steam (some niche genres have limited widget representation).

## Evidence
"How Many Dudes?" case study (7-person team, 1-year development):
- Launch wishlists: 308K
- Units sold since July 30 launch: 242,000
- Demo median playtime: 2 hours 2 minutes
- New & Trending Free widget: 36,946 wishlists from 24 days on the widget
- YouTube Shorts: 769 wishlists

> "The wishlists that come from the Steam algorithm like New & Trending Free, The Discovery Queue will dwarf anything you can do with social media."

## Signals
- Demo median playtime above 90 minutes suggests the game will qualify for algorithmic promotion and hold attention
- Wishlist velocity accelerates after the demo appears in Steam rather than after social media pushes
- YouTube Shorts drives view and comment engagement metrics but the wishlist-to-view ratio remains low; Steam widget drives fewer raw impressions but converts at a far higher rate

## Counter-evidence
The "How Many Dudes?" case involves a resonant, well-designed game that would likely perform well through any discovery path. A low-quality demo with poor playtime will not earn prominent widget placement regardless of the demo format. The New & Trending Free widget position is competitive; not all demos receive front-page placement. Games in genres with saturated Steam competition face heavier filters before reaching prominent widget slots.
