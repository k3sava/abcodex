# Festival genre fit predicts indie game visibility ROI more reliably than total festival count

## Claim
Appearing in many third-party Steam festivals does not reliably translate to reviews or sales; the genre match between a game and a festival's curatorial lens is the limiting factor.

## Mechanism
Virtual 3rd-party festivals curate heavily by genre. Cozy games, city builders, management sims, and narrative experiences dominate festival selection committees, while roguelites, simulation, and horror remain underrepresented relative to their Steam market share. A game entered in festivals mismatched to its genre accumulates appearances without converting them into wishlists or reviews, because the browsing audience attending that festival is not the audience predisposed to buy the game.

Publisher relationships also compound the distribution inequality. Games with dedicated marketing teams and genre-festival alignment capture disproportionate festival slots, while solo developers applying indiscriminately dilute their own returns.

Zukowski draws the diagnostic conclusion clearly: "The limiting factor is not visibility, it is finding a match with the Steam audience."

## Conditions
Holds when: the developer is targeting third-party festivals as a primary pre-launch discovery channel. Applies across the 470 festivals Zukowski tracked since 2019. Fails when: a game genre is itself underrepresented in the festival ecosystem, in which case no genre-matched festival exists and a different marketing channel should be prioritized.

## Evidence
Zukowski tracked 470 third-party festivals and 31,285 unique games appearing in at least one festival, representing 31% of all Steam releases since 2020. The median game appeared in 2.91 festivals. The 95th percentile appeared in 10. The top 1% accumulated 50 or more appearances.

Lost Twins 2, the clearest counterexample in the dataset, appeared in more than 35 festivals and earned only 53 reviews, showing that volume of festival exposure uncorrelated with genre fit produces negligible commercial outcome.

## Signals
- Games in the 50th percentile of festival appearances earn similar review counts to games in the 1st percentile when genre match is weak.
- Developer time spent applying to genre-mismatched festivals yields lower per-application ROI than applying selectively to well-curated genre-relevant events.
- Publisher-backed games in the same genre consistently appear in more genre-matched festivals, widening the discoverability gap.

## Counter-evidence
Zukowski excludes Valve-run events (Steam Next Fest) and themed sales from this dataset, and acknowledges incomplete historical coverage due to removed festival pages. Survivorship bias applies: only games appearing in at least one festival are tracked, so true zero-festival outcomes are absent from the analysis. Some games outside the dominant genre clusters still attract significant festival representation through community-driven visibility.
