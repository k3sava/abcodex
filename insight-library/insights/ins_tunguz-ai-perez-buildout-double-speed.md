# AI is traversing the Perez build-out cycle twice as fast as the web era did

## Claim
Application revenue reached 10% of AI infrastructure capex in four years; the web took seven years to reach the same milestone, placing the AI era about two cycles ahead of where the internet was at an equivalent point.

## Mechanism
Carlota Perez's model of technology build-outs holds that infrastructure capital runs far ahead of application revenue, then application growth catches up and pays for the build. The gap closes faster in AI than in telecom because: applications build on APIs rather than physical infrastructure, model capabilities improve on a faster iteration cycle than network capacity did, and the developer tooling for AI applications was already mature when the build-out began. A shorter gap means the infrastructure pays back sooner and the risk of unsupported capex is lower.

## Conditions
Holds when: Comparing AI infrastructure to the 1996-2005 telecom boom using Perez's two-phase framework. The metric is ratio of application revenue to infrastructure capex, not total revenue.

Fails when: AI spending circularity inflates the application-revenue numerator without representing real end-customer demand. Ramp data show 80% of the largest AI spenders are venture-backed, and VC-backed companies now exceed 13% of US employment, meaning much application revenue circulates within the startup ecosystem rather than flowing from outside it.

## Evidence
Web era (1996-2005): approximately $1.5T of telecom capex against approximately $180B of application revenue. Application revenue took seven years to reach 10% of capex.

AI era: application revenue reached 10% of capex in four years.

> "Every major technology build-out requires two phases"

Tunguz cites SpaceX S-1 data showing compute payback in under one year at spot prices. Venture investment in AI's first four years reached approximately $1.24T, versus approximately $201.8B for the web's first four years.

## Signals
- Application-layer ARR in AI-native categories grows faster than comparable web-era application ARR at the same number of years from infrastructure build-out
- Large cloud providers report compute payback periods under two years for new data center deployments
- AI application companies reach $100M ARR faster than comparable SaaS companies did in 2000-2005

## Counter-evidence
Ramp's sample is skewed toward tech and excludes free tools and personal-account AI use, which Ramp estimates at about 40% of AI application spend. The circularity concern is structural: if 80% of large AI spenders are venture-backed and venture-backed firms are 13% of US employment, a significant portion of AI application revenue is startups buying from other startups, not from the broader economy. That would overstate true penetration. Tunguz himself flags this as an open question rather than a resolved one.
