# Post-purchase behavior builds the AI search shortlist, not on-page content signals

## Claim
AI search builds product shortlists from post-purchase signals (reviews, YouTube mentions, brand searches) that correlate at 0.6 to 0.7 with shortlist inclusion, while traditional SEO inputs like domain rating (0.3) and referring domains (0.3) are weaker predictors.

## Mechanism
AI search trains on signals of social proof and brand recall that accumulate after customers buy. A customer who purchases then reviews, mentions on YouTube, or searches by brand feeds those signals back into the system AI uses to construct its shortlists. Third-party signals reflecting what customers did post-purchase correlate far more strongly with AI visibility than the on-page or link inputs that drove classic search. The path to shortlist inclusion runs through post-purchase experience design, not meta tags.

Indig's correlation data from Ahrefs (against AI Overview visibility): YouTube mentions (0.7), branded web mentions (0.7), branded anchors (0.6), branded search volume (0.4). Versus domain rating (0.3), referring domains (0.3).

## Conditions
Holds when: the product category has meaningful repeat purchase rates and community activity; AI search volumes are significant enough to affect revenue; third-party platforms (YouTube, Reddit, review sites) are active for the category.

Fails when: the product has no natural post-purchase review community; the category is niche enough that AI shortlists are driven by a handful of authoritative sources rather than distributed signals; brand is too new to have accumulated purchase history.

## Evidence
Indig published analysis from his own AI Mode user study and Ahrefs correlation research.

- Participants in his AI Mode study: 74% picked the top-ranked product, 88% took the shortlist as-is without verifying elsewhere.
- ChatGPT shopping sessions: 93% had no meaningful click-out, and only 3% verified answers on the open web.
- 39% of US consumers have used AI for shopping (Adobe data); 11% let AI make purchases for them (Gartner).
- AI influenced 20% of purchases during 2025 BFCM (Salesforce).
- Ridge CMO quoted in the piece: "Probably 70% of our sales come from direct, organic, or branded search."

> "the shortlist gets built from what your customers do after they buy"

## Signals
- Third-party review volume growing organically without paid prompting
- YouTube mentions of the brand appearing without direct outreach
- Branded search volume increasing in GSC month-over-month
- Comments and community posts referencing the brand spontaneously

## Counter-evidence
Brand recognition can override rank: in the same AI Mode study, shoppers cited brand recognition as a trust signal in 34% of sessions versus 23% in classic search, suggesting high-recognition brands can shortcut the post-purchase signal loop. Post-purchase signals don't appear in last-click reporting, making them structurally underinvested. The correlation data comes from Ahrefs and may reflect the specific AI search surfaces (AI Overviews) studied rather than all AI shopping surfaces.
