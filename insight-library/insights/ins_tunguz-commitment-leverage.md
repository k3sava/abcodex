# AI foundation model companies carry their financial risk in compute commitments, not debt

## Claim
AI foundation model companies have shifted financial risk from debt to long-duration compute commitments, creating a risk structure with no historical parallel in tech. Standard valuation frameworks built around debt-to-equity miss the exposure entirely.

## Mechanism
Traditional software companies carry risk in debt instruments that can be renegotiated or refinanced. AI infrastructure commitments are non-cancelable, multi-year, and often two-thirds of total exposure. Revenue is consumption-based with no contract lock-in. The commitments pay out whether customers renew or not. A revenue slowdown does not reduce the obligation. Analysts evaluating AI foundation model companies need a different vocabulary for financial risk.

## Conditions
Holds when: AI companies pre-commit to compute capacity at scale; revenue is priced per token with no long-term customer contracts.
Fails when: AI compute costs fall faster than commitments lock in rates; companies negotiate cancellation provisions into agreements; consumption-based revenue develops into contracted recurring revenue.

## Evidence
Tunguz calculates Anthropic's total compute commitments across seven named agreements at $637B, with approximately two-thirds non-cancelable. The Anthropic S-1 discloses $265.6B in fixed or minimum guarantees at June 30, 2026, plus $245B executed since. Outstanding debt is $6.2B in non-interest-bearing Amazon convertibles. The $15B revolver is undrawn. Net revenue retention is approximately 900%. No single customer exceeded 10% of revenue in the nine months to September 30, 2026.

> "The leverage is instead in commitments."

> "revenue is consumption-based with no lock-in & the payments arrive whether customers do."

Note: the $637B total and the gross margin figures Tunguz cites (approximately 40% for 2025, 85% for H1 2026) are his own calculations from cost-of-revenue line items, not Anthropic disclosures.

## Signals
- AI company S-1s reporting large minimum compute guarantees with non-cancelable terms
- Credit analysts shifting focus to commitment schedules rather than traditional debt metrics
- AI infrastructure providers requiring multi-year agreements as preconditions for capacity allocation

## Counter-evidence
High net revenue retention (900%) and lack of customer concentration above 10% mitigate the commitment risk in Anthropic's case. If revenue growth continues at its H1 2026 pace, the commitment schedule is manageable. Tunguz himself presents the case as two-sided.
