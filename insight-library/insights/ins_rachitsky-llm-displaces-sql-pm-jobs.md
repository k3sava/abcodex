# LLM skills now top PM job descriptions, overtaking SQL and Python as companies signal what the role requires

## Claim
In late 2026, "LLM" became the most-mentioned skill in PM job descriptions, surpassing SQL, Jira, AWS, and Python. Claude ranked third. For designers, Claude is now the second most-mentioned skill, behind Figma but ahead of CSS.

## Mechanism
Job description keywords track the operational requirements companies write to the current bar. When AI tools handle the tasks previously done with SQL, Python, and task-tracking software, companies update their job posts to reflect what actually matters for the role. AI fluency shifts from differentiator to entry requirement. The delay between practice changing and job descriptions updating is roughly one hiring cycle, so by the time "LLM" tops the keyword list, the operational shift has already happened inside teams. The same pattern holds for design: a tool's appearance in a job description signals that it already decides who can contribute.

## Conditions
Holds when: AI tools are actively embedded in day-to-day PM and design workflows, not just mentioned in onboarding.
Fails when: companies post "LLM" as a signal of forward-looking culture without changing actual job tasks; in that case the keyword reflects aspiration, not requirement. Supply-constrained roles may also list desired skills that few candidates possess, making the keyword measure scarcity rather than practice.

## Evidence
Rachitsky's late-2026 job market report analyzed open roles globally. The data showed "LLM" reached the top position in PM skill mentions, with Claude at third. A year prior, SQL and Python led. For designers, Claude has displaced CSS as the second most-mentioned skill. The report covers U.S. and global open postings; exact sample size is not disclosed in the public version.

## Signals
- PM job posts now include AI tool assessment criteria alongside product sense and prioritization.
- Candidates who cannot describe LLM use in daily work are screened out at intake.
- Design job posts have dropped Sketch and CSS from top five mentions, replaced by Claude and other AI tools.

## Counter-evidence
Job description keywords lag actual practice by months and reflect employer signaling as much as job requirements. A company posting "LLM" may still hire for traditional PM skills and expect AI upskilling after onboarding. The trend may also reflect a supply gap: companies signal LLM fluency precisely because few candidates demonstrate it, not because current teams depend on it uniformly.
