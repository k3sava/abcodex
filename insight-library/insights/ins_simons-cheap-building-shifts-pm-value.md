# Cheap building removes the old rationing system that made PM gatekeeping valuable

## Claim
When building is cheap, the PM role shifts from gatekeeping scarce engineering to closing the customer insight loop. The only thing that cannot be replicated by cheap builders is proximity to customers.

## Mechanism
Product management as a discipline emerged when engineering hours were the scarce resource. Writing specs, prioritizing backlogs, and deciding what not to build were high-value activities because someone had to ration the thing that was expensive. Gatekeeping was the job.

Cheap building removes the constraint. When a PM or a non-technical person can ship a working prototype in hours, the prioritization function loses most of its value. Anyone can build. Deciding what to build next is no longer a bottleneck worth organizing a team around.

What remains is customer context. Understanding what users actually need, why they behave the way they do, and what "better" looks like for them requires sustained proximity to real users. That knowledge cannot be generated by a model or replicated by a team that hasn't done the work. PMs who shift from managing a backlog to running tight customer insight loops become the ones whose judgment drives which cheap builds are worth shipping.

## Conditions
Holds when: building costs have fallen enough that engineering scarcity is no longer the binding constraint on output; the team has access to sufficient building capacity to act on customer insight quickly.

Fails when: engineering remains genuinely scarce (legacy systems, specialized domains); the product is in a regulated environment where rapid prototyping is not viable; the team lacks reliable access to real customers and defaults to assumption-based building regardless.

## Evidence
Simons presented at Mind the Product Chicago in October 2026. Conference notes published October 12, 2026 describe his argument: cheap building removes the old rationing system that made engineering time scarce. PMs shift from writing specs to building prototypes, from gatekeeping backlogs to closing customer-insight loops, and their advantage shifts to customer context, which cannot be copied. The source is a conference write-up, not a transcript, so specific wording may not be verbatim.

## Signals
- PMs are spending more time with users than in planning meetings
- Prototypes replace written specs in planning conversations
- The team ships and discards multiple small builds in the time it used to take to spec one feature
- Customer insight cycles shorten from weeks to days

## Counter-evidence
This is Simons' stated view from a single conference talk, reported through a conference write-up rather than a primary source. The framework assumes building costs fall fast enough that they stop being the bottleneck, which varies by company stage and technical context. Legacy systems, compliance requirements, and integration complexity keep engineering scarce in many organizations regardless of AI tooling advances.
