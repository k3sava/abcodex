# Counter-positioning works as a startup moat because incumbents face profit cannibalization if they copy, not because startups have technical advantages

## Claim
Counter-positioning is the one moat a young startup can build before it earns network effects or scale economies. It works not because the startup is better, but because copying the startup's business model would directly cannibalize the incumbent's existing profit streams.

## Mechanism
The startup adopts a fundamentally different business model than the incumbent. If the incumbent copies the startup's approach, it undercuts the pricing, revenue structure, or distribution that the incumbent's existing customers and margins depend on. The incumbent faces a choice: maintain current profits or fight back. Most choose to maintain current profits, giving the startup time to build other moats. The window is temporary. Startups must use it to accumulate data, distribution, and customer relationships before incumbents find a way to adapt or acquire.

The concept originates with Hamilton Helmer's "7 Powers." McCormick's September 2026 essay applies it to current AI-era companies and argues it is the most accessible power for early-stage startups precisely because it requires no scale, no network, and no technology advantage to activate.

## Conditions
Holds when: the incumbent's core revenue depends on a model that would be undermined by copying; the incumbent is rational enough to preserve short-term profits over long-term competitive response; the startup can use the window to build a second moat before the incumbent adapts.

Fails when: the incumbent is willing to accept short-term margin pain to eliminate the threat; the startup's business model is easily replicated without cannibalization; the incumbent acquires the startup before any second moat forms; or the startup misreads the window as permanent.

## Evidence
Three examples McCormick uses:

Ramp positioned itself around helping customers spend less. Corporate card incumbents earn revenue from transaction volume and interchange fees. Copying Ramp's spend-reduction model would directly cut their own income.

Base Power shifted from selling batteries to selling electricity. Incumbent battery companies would have to restructure their entire business model to compete.

Facebook launched Harvard-only rather than maximizing early users, which contradicted MySpace's growth-at-all-costs approach. MySpace matching that strategy would have required it to slow its own growth, which its model and investors demanded it not do.

## Signals
- Incumbent publicly dismisses your model as unsustainable or irrelevant rather than matching it
- Incumbent launches a half-measure or separate brand rather than changing its core product
- Your early customers are the incumbent's least-profitable customers (the ones it is quietly happy to lose)
- You can raise prices or expand scope before the incumbent responds

## Counter-evidence
Counter-positioning is explicitly a temporary moat. McCormick acknowledges it must be used to build durable advantages. Startups that ride counter-positioning without building a second moat lose when the incumbent finally accepts margin pain or finds a non-cannibalistic way to compete. Google and Microsoft have both crossed this threshold at various points in their histories, accepting short-term losses to neutralize counter-positioned threats.
