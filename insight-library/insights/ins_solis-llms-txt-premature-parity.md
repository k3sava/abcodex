# Treating unproven AI-era signals at the same priority as proven technical fixes misallocates the engineering capacity that is the actual constraint

## Claim
When only 28% of SEO teams implement 80% or more of their own recommendations, engineering capacity is already the binding constraint. Giving new, unproven AI-era signals like llms.txt equal priority to fixes with years of multi-site evidence means well-evidenced work gets displaced, not just deprioritized.

## Mechanism
Traditional technical SEO fixes (JavaScript rendering, crawl efficiency, structured data) have accumulated multi-site evidence over years. Teams have a reasonable basis for estimating their impact in a given context. New AI-era signals, including llms.txt and emerging LLM crawl directives, lack that evidence base. No multi-site study exists that shows what impact llms.txt implementation has on AI search referrals at a given scale or category.

The prioritization error is not in testing new signals. It is in treating them as equivalent to proven fixes before testing. When dev capacity is finite and already under-deployed (28% implementation rate), equal prioritization is not neutral: it crowds out work with higher expected return. The correct sequence is to pilot-test new signals, establish whether they have measurable impact in a specific context, and then allocate capacity based on measured evidence.

> "Giving Llms.txt & js the same priority shows we haven't learned."

Solis presented this at TechSEOConnect on November 13, 2026, as part of her broader analysis of the shifts in technical SEO priorities for the AI search era.

## Conditions
Holds when: engineering capacity is already the binding constraint on SEO implementation; the new signal has no published multi-site impact evidence; the team is operating in a category or scale where the signal's behavior is likely to differ from early case studies.

Fails when: a specific site has already resolved its traditional technical debt and dev capacity is genuinely available; or when the new signal has accumulated enough evidence from similar sites to support a probability-weighted prioritization decision.

## Evidence
Solis cited the 28% implementation rate across SEO teams as a measure of the execution gap, a figure she has tracked across her consulting work and referenced in multiple talks through 2026. The TechSEOConnect deck included side-by-side prioritization examples contrasting JavaScript rendering (high evidence, high capacity cost) with llms.txt (low evidence, low capacity cost) to illustrate that treating them as equivalent is a category error.

## Signals
- An SEO roadmap that includes llms.txt at the same sprint priority as JS rendering fixes, without pilot-test results supporting parity, is the pattern Solis is warning against.
- Teams that test llms.txt in a controlled way (pilot site, defined metric, comparison period) can legitimately promote or deprioritize it based on what they find.
- Implementation backlogs with low completion rates are evidence that capacity, not ideation, is the binding constraint.
