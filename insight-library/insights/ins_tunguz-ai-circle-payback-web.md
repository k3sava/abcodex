# AI application revenue is reaching the infrastructure payback threshold twice as fast as the web era did

## Claim
AI application revenue reached 10% of infrastructure capex in four years; the web era took seven. The main risk is that 80% of the largest AI spenders are venture-backed, making the demand circle narrow.

## Mechanism
Carlota Perez's framework says every major technology build-out runs in two phases: infrastructure first, then the applications that pay for it back. The faster applications generate revenue relative to infrastructure spend, the sooner the build-out proves sustainable. AI is running that cycle at double the web era's speed. The risk is circularity: if startups funded by venture capital are buying AI services from other venture-funded companies, the demand signal may reflect capital recycling rather than durable end-customer revenue.

## Conditions
Holds when: AI application revenue continues to grow faster than infrastructure spend, and the venture-funded demand base expands beyond itself.
Fails when: VC funding tightens and the startups that represent 80% of large AI spend cut back together, causing a demand collapse that is not offset by enterprise or consumer revenue.

## Evidence
Tunguz uses PitchBook data showing US venture invested $516 billion in the first nine months of 2026, 44% above the full-year 2021 record. Ramp data shows 80% of the largest AI spenders are venture-backed. Venture-backed firms now account for more than 13% of US employment, up from about 5% in the web era. SpaceX's S-1 states that new compute deployments are generating a payback of less than a year. The web era took seven years for application revenue to exceed 10% of capex; AI hit 10% in four.

> "AI has reached that 10% mark in four years, roughly half the time."

> "The circularity is real, but the circle is a sixth of the economy."

## Signals
- AI application revenue as a percentage of total AI infrastructure capex crosses 25% before 2028.
- Share of AI spending from non-VC-backed buyers (enterprise, consumer, government) rises above 30%.
- Compute payback periods stay below two years across the major providers.

## Counter-evidence
Tunguz raises the ouroboros risk himself: if most AI spend is startups buying from other startups, the demand signal is fragile. The Ramp data measures funding status of spenders but not whether their own customers are venture-backed, understating true circularity. Free tools and personal-account AI use account for roughly 40% of application spend and are excluded from the Ramp data, which could mean the paying circle is even narrower. The web era comparison uses different data sources across eras, limiting precision.
