Eric Simons co-founded StackBlitz, the browser-based development environment that became Bolt, one of the first widely adopted AI app-building platforms. His position at the intersection of cheap building tools and product practice makes him a practitioner source on how product management changes when engineering costs fall sharply. He speaks and writes from the vantage of someone who shipped the tool that lowered building costs, then watched how teams changed in response.

## Operating themes
- **Cheap building changes what product work means.** Simons argues that when anyone can build, the scarce resource shifts from engineering hours to customer understanding. The PM advantage moves to whoever maintains closest proximity to real users.
- **Prototypes replace specs.** With fast building, a working prototype is cheaper to produce than a detailed spec and gives more information. The spec-writing phase shrinks or disappears.
- **Customer insight loops as the defensible core.** What cannot be replicated by cheap building tools is sustained, direct knowledge of why customers behave the way they do. That knowledge is the new gating constraint.
