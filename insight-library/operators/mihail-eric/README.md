Mihail Eric teaches CS146S, "The Modern Software Developer," as adjunct professor at Stanford University, and leads AI efforts at Monaco. His Stanford course trains engineers to work with coding agents on real production codebases, requiring students to contribute patches to open-source projects under maintainer review. He writes on the new bar for entry-level engineers, the buy-vs-build decision in AI tooling, and the limits of delegating comprehension to agents.

## Operating themes
- **Comprehension as the prerequisite for delegation** Engineers cannot reliably review or extend code they do not understand, regardless of how well the agent generated it.
- **Buy before you build in AI tooling** Purchasing tools builds domain knowledge at low cost. Building makes sense only once gaps are real and the team understands the problem well.
- **Spec-writing as the durable human skill** As generation becomes cheaper, the irreducible human advantage shifts to the ability to describe precisely what should be built and why.
