Gary King is a political scientist and applied statistician at Harvard University, where he holds one of the university's named professorships and directs the Institute for Quantitative Social Science. He has spent decades building statistical methods for causal inference and has produced widely used open-source software for the social sciences. His October 2026 working paper with Steven Worthington, "AI Article Reviews Dominate Human Peer Reviews at Their Best," ran a controlled comparison of AI and human peer reviews across 124 papers and found Claude Opus 4.8 outperformed expert human reviewers on every measured dimension. The paper argues that the results carry direct policy implications for how academic journals should integrate AI into their workflows.

## Operating themes
- **Causal inference as the lens.** King's methods work starts from the question of what constitutes valid causal evidence, and his AI research applies the same standard: measure AI review quality with the same rigor used to assess any intervention.
- **Empirical evidence over assertion.** His claims are grounded in controlled studies with explicit methodology, not expert opinion or benchmark-adjacent claims.
- **Policy implications from research.** King does not stop at findings; he draws explicit policy recommendations for how institutions should adapt, including proposed changes to journal review processes.
