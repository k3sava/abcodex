Michael Lynch. Bootstrapped founder who spent a decade as a software engineer at Google and Microsoft before leaving to build TinyPilot, a remote KVM device company, entirely in public. His blog at mtlynch.io is a multi-year archive of unvarnished founding decisions: year-end retrospectives with exact revenue and expense figures, pricing experiments, product postmortems, and technical deep dives. He applied the same rigor to writing with his book Refactoring English, a practical guide to cleaner technical prose.

His analysis of coding agents is practitioner diagnosis, not demo criticism. He uses multiple coding agents daily in his own workflow. The failures he documents are the ones he encounters before breakfast.

## Operating themes
- **Daily use as diagnostic method** Claims anchored in his own production workflow. He does not evaluate from benchmarks or keynotes.
- **Separation of what is built from what is measured** Consistent attention to whether the metric tracks the thing that actually matters. Benchmarks measure models; agent task-management quality is largely unmeasured.
- **Security as a design constraint, not a policy** Access control code is deterministic. It cannot defer to agent judgment.
