Katie Dill. Head of Design at Stripe, where she leads product and brand design across one of the highest-craft fintech companies building today. Dill's work at Stripe and her earlier work at Lyft and Airbnb have made her one of the most referenced practitioners on what it means to maintain quality standards as organizations scale. Her "raise the ceiling" framing came from a direct observation: as AI makes basic product building accessible to everyone, the differentiator shifts to the teams that use AI to reach quality levels they could not have shipped before, not just to ship standard quality faster.

## Operating themes
- **Ceiling-raising over floor-lifting** Dill's central argument is that AI's most valuable design use is achieving previously-unattainable quality, not just accelerating adequate quality. Teams that optimize only for speed converge on generic.
- **Taste as the non-compressible input** Dill argues that a clear point of view about what makes a product worth building is the defense against zombie UI: product experiences that look similar because they were generated by the same AI tools without distinct judgment.
- **Anticipation as the quality signal** What users notice most is not feature completeness but the detail that shows the product anticipated their need. Dill uses this as the test for whether AI is being used to raise or just floor.
