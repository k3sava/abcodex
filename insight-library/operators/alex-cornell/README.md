Alex Cornell. Design Director at Meta AI, leading design for Muse. Cornell brings a human-to-human conversational frame to AI product design: the core question he asks when designing AI interactions is how the exchange would work between two people, not how a software interface would handle it. He applied this frame to remove traditional chat UI patterns from Muse and to restructure the product around a single persistent thread.

## Operating themes
- **Fixed structure, dynamic content** Cornell separates what designers control (structural containers, component types) from what AI generates (text, suggestions, status signals), keeping design systems intact while allowing personalization.
- **Speed eliminates UX workarounds** Features designed to manage latency anxiety, such as chain-of-thought display, become noise once models get fast enough to deliver results directly.
- **Progressive disclosure by audience** The same AI activity shown at three detail levels, emoji, task list, and raw logs, serves casual users, engaged users, and engineers without creating three separate interfaces.
