Wes Kao. Management educator and writer focused on the practical craft of communicating clearly, managing up, and delegating without losing control of outcomes. Known for a bi-weekly newsletter that breaks down the mechanics of high-performance work: how to give feedback that lands, how to run meetings that produce decisions, how to delegate without babysitting.

Her body of work treats management as a learnable skill, not a personality trait. Each piece isolates a specific failure mode and gives a concrete repair. The delegation framework is characteristic of this approach: rather than a principle ("be clearer"), it produces a six-question checklist that the delegator runs before every handoff.

## Operating themes
- **Pre-delegation context transfer** Delegation failures almost always originate with the delegator omitting context, not with the delegatee lacking skill.
- **Managing up as a learnable craft** Executives who are hard to work with are often just missing specific communication protocols, not missing talent.
- **Scope before speed** When output arrives slower or worse than expected, the root cause is almost always undefined scope, not insufficient effort.
